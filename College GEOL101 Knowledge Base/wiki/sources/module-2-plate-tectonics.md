Module 2 covers the development of the theory of plate tectonics, from Wegener's continental drift hypothesis through seafloor spreading to the modern theory. Covers all three boundary types, paleomagnetism, hot spots, and plate-driving mechanisms. Instructor: Dr. Brandi Lenz. Includes slides contributed by Michelle Chrpa (paleomagnetism sections).

## Topics Covered

### Continental Drift
- Until the late 1960s most geologists believed continents were fixed.
- **Alfred Wegener** (1915): published *The Origin of Continents and Oceans*.
  - Proposed supercontinent **Pangaea** ("all lands") began fragmenting ~200 Ma (Mesozoic).
  - Evidence: (1) continental fit (jigsaw puzzle), (2) matching rock records, (3) fossil record (identical organisms on multiple continents), (4) matching mountain ranges across the North Atlantic, (5) glacial and coal-swamp location distributions.
- Rejected by the scientific community — Wegener could not identify a credible mechanism (incorrectly invoked Moon/Sun gravity and icebreaker analogy).

### Seafloor Spreading
- Post-WWII oceanographic exploration revealed:
  - Oceanic ridge system winds through all major oceans (>70,000 km long).
  - No oceanic crust older than 180 Ma.
  - Thin sediment accumulation in deep oceans.
- **Harry Hess**: proposed seafloor spreading — new ocean floor generated at mid-ocean ridges.
- Rift valley runs along the crest of ridges.
- Spreading rates: average 5 cm/yr; Mid-Atlantic Ridge ~2 cm/yr; East Pacific Rise ~15 cm/yr.

### Theory of Plate Tectonics
- Lithosphere (crust + upper mantle) broken into numerous **lithospheric plates**.
- Plates "float" on weak, hot **asthenosphere**.
- Plates move ~3 cm/yr and interact at **plate boundaries**.
- Interactions produce earthquakes, volcanoes, mountains, ocean basins.

### Plate Boundary Types

#### Divergent Boundaries (Constructive)
- Two plates move apart; new ocean floor generated.
- Most occur at mid-ocean ridges.
- **Continental rifting**: divergence within a continent → elongated depression → narrow sea → new ocean basin.
  - Example: East African Rift / Mt. Kilimanjaro.
- Example of surface expression: Iceland.

#### Convergent Boundaries
- Two plates move together. Features: subduction zones, folded mountain ranges, volcanoes, earthquake zones.
- **Oceanic–continental convergence**: denser oceanic slab subducts; water from subducting plate triggers partial melting → **continental volcanic arc** (e.g., Andes, Cascade Range).
- **Oceanic–oceanic convergence**: one slab descends; partial melting → **volcanic island arc** (e.g., Aleutian Islands, Mariana Islands). Deep-ocean trench forms.
- **Continental–continental convergence**: less-dense continental lithosphere does not subduct → **mountain belts of deformed rock** (e.g., Himalayas, Alps, Appalachians).
- Deep-ocean trenches: Peru-Chile Trench, Mariana Trench, Tonga Trench.
- Subduction depth for partial melting trigger: ~100 km.

#### Transform Boundaries
- Plates slide horizontally past one another — no crust created or destroyed.
- Most occur on seafloor joining two spreading centers (fracture zones).
- Continental examples: **San Andreas Fault** (California), Alpine Fault (New Zealand).

### Changing Plate Boundaries
- Earth's total surface area does not change, but individual plate sizes/shapes do.
- Plate boundaries migrate, are created and destroyed.
- Breakup of Pangaea → Atlantic Ocean basin; India–Asia collision → Himalayas.
- Future projections: Baja/S. California will slide past North American Plate; Africa continues colliding with Eurasia.

### Summary Points
- Continental lithosphere: thicker, less dense.
- Oceanic lithosphere: thinner, denser — only oceanic lithosphere can normally be subducted.
- New ocean lithosphere created at mid-ocean ridges; destroyed at subduction zones.
- Plate boundaries = major loci of volcanoes and earthquakes.

### Paleomagnetism and Marine Magnetic Anomalies
- Earth has a **dipolar magnetic field** (like a bar magnet); poles differ from geographic poles.
- **Declination**: difference between magnetic north and geographic north.
- **Inclination**: tilt of compass needle from horizontal.
- **Paleomagnetism**: iron-rich minerals align to the magnetic field when rocks form → preserve declination and inclination → record the latitude and longitude of rock formation.
- **Apparent Polar Wander**: plotting paleo-pole positions over time; comparing two continents shows rotated paths that align when continents are restored → evidence for continental motion.
- **Magnetic reversals**: Earth's field periodically reverses polarity.
  - Normal polarity: N geographic = N magnetic.
  - Reversed polarity: N geographic = S magnetic.
- **Marine magnetic anomalies**: symmetric striped pattern of normal/reversed polarity parallel to mid-ocean ridges — the "paleomagnetic tape recorder." Magnetometer confirms seafloor spreading.
- Ocean drilling: distribution and thickness of sediments provides additional verification.

### Hot Spots and Mantle Plumes
- **Mantle plume**: cylindrical upwelling of hot rock.
- **Hot spot**: surface volcanic expression of a mantle plume.
- As a plate moves over a stationary hot spot → **hot-spot track** (chain of volcanoes with age increasing away from hot spot).
- Examples: Hawaiian Island chain (oceanic), Yellowstone (continental).
- Hot spots do not occur at plate boundaries; thought stationary over long geologic time.

### What Drives Plate Motion?
- **Slab-pull force**: subduction of cold, dense oceanic lithosphere pulls the rest of the plate.
- **Ridge-push force**: elevated lithosphere at ridges slides down due to gravity.
- **Plate-mantle convection**: plate tectonics and convective flow in the mantle are part of the same system; energy source is Earth's internal heat.
