Laboratory worksheet covering the latitude–longitude coordinate system, Google Earth, topographic map construction, and relief/gradient analysis. Corresponds to Chapter 9 of the laboratory manual (activity sheets 9.1, 9.3, 9.5; textbook pages 253–262). Source is a scanned, image-only PDF — no text layer — so all content was read from page images.

## Activities Covered

### Activity 9.1 — Map and Google Earth Inquiry (pp. 253–254)
Applies the latitude–longitude system to locate a country, a quadrangle, and a place of the student's choosing, then explores it in Google Earth. Introduces degrees–minutes–seconds notation, elevation readout, layers, the ruler tool, and (Pro desktop only) historical imagery. Closes by asking how Google Earth could be used to study the geology of a region.

Key figure: **Fig. A9.1.1**, a world map with the graticule drawn at 30° intervals. The shaded quadrangle is bounded by the **Equator and 30° S** and by **60° E and 90° E** — the central Indian Ocean, east of Madagascar and west of Sumatra.

### Activity 9.3 — Topographic Map Construction (p. 257)
Hands-on contouring practice.

- **Fig. A9.3.1** — interpolate unlabeled spot elevations and contour at a **100 ft** interval. Highland centered on benchmark **BM675**; land falls to an ocean at lower right. Because 675 ft is the summit, **600 is the highest contour that can be drawn**.
- **Fig. A9.3.2** — a 5 × 4 grid of spot elevations contoured at a **10 ft** interval (contours at 80, 90, 100, 110, 120). A stream valley runs down the middle column; elevations there read 78 → 84 → 98 → 110 from top to bottom, so **the stream flows toward the top of the map** and all contour V's point toward the bottom. The `110` value in row 2, column 4 is a **saddle** — lower ground lies both above and below it.
- **Fig. A9.3.3** — label pre-drawn contours at a **10 ft** interval starting from sea level. Transects from the shoreline inland give **0, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70**. Two hachured depressions require the repeat-then-decrease rule.

### Activity 9.5 — Relief and Gradient (Slope) Analysis (pp. 261–262)
Quantitative work on **Fig. A9.5.1**, a USGS contour map of the Merced River area, Yosemite.

| Quantity | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Contour interval | **40 ft** | Index contours 6200/6400/6600 are 200 ft apart; every 5th line is an index contour. Confirmed by counting exactly 4 intermediate rings between index lines on the knob. |
| Elevation of B | **6560–6600 ft** | Inside the innermost 6560 ring; no 6600 ring present |
| Elevation of X | **6440–6480 ft** | 3 contours below B |
| Elevation of Y | **6200–6240 ft** | 9 contours below B |
| Elevation of A | **~6160–6200 ft** | Beside the Merced River, just below the 6200 contour (least certain figure) |
| Distance A→B | **≈ 2400 ft** | A–B measures 2.41 × the 1000 ft bar scale |
| Distance X→Y | **≈ 500 ft** | 0.50 × the bar scale |
| Gradient A→B | **≈ 0.17 ft/ft** | 400 ft ÷ 2400 ft |
| Gradient X→Y | **≈ 0.48 ft/ft** | 240 ft ÷ 500 ft |

The X–Y face is about **three times steeper** than the straight-line A–B average, which the worksheet itself asserts. Steepest ground on the map is the **west-to-southwest flank of the knob** below B.

The closing discussion asks for a gentle ~1-mile route from A to B that always climbs. A switchback route crossing contours obliquely, staying west of the drainage east of the knob, runs about 5600 ft at **0.071 ft/ft** — same 400 ft of climb, roughly 40% the steepness of the direct line.

## Notes and Gaps
- Activity **9.3 part D** references **Figs. 9.1B–C**, which are *textbook* figures (no `A` prefix) and are not present in the worksheet PDF. The method still applies: count contour **spaces** between two labeled contours and divide the elevation difference by that count.
- Activities 9.2 and 9.4 are not present in this scan.
- Several items (home place, Google Earth measurements, pencil contouring) require the student's own input or physical measurement and have no fixed answer.
