Module 8 introduces the geologic time scale and the two main methods geologists use to date rocks and events: relative age dating (placing events in sequence) and numerical (absolute) age dating (assigning specific ages in years). The module covers Steno's foundational principles, unconformities, fossils and correlation, radiometric decay, and alternative dating methods.

## Section 1: Origin and Early Evolution of Earth

- The universe began with the **Big Bang**; all solar system bodies formed from the same rotating cloud of gas and dust — the **solar nebula**.
- **Nebular theory**: the nebula collapsed under gravity, spun faster, flattened into a disc; most mass condensed into the proto-Sun; inner planets formed from rocky/metallic material; outer planets from ices (H₂O, CO₂).
- As early Earth accumulated mass, temperatures were high enough to melt iron and nickel → **chemical differentiation**: metals sank to form the core; lighter molten rock rose to form a primitive crust; established the core–mantle–crust divisions.
- A primitive atmosphere evolved from volcanic outgassing.

## Section 2: The Geologic Time Scale

- Organizes Earth's ~4.56 billion year history into eons, eras, periods, and epochs.
- Built by combining relative dating principles with numerical dates from radiometric dating.
- See [[Geologic Time]] for the full conceptual treatment.

## Section 3: Relative Age Dating Principles

Developed before radiometric techniques; still valid and widely used.

### Principle of Superposition (Steno, 1669)
In an **undeformed** sequence of sedimentary rocks, each layer is older than the one above and younger than the one below.

### Principle of Original Horizontality
Sediment layers are deposited nearly horizontally. Tilted or folded strata have been deformed after deposition.

### Principle of Lateral Continuity
Sedimentary beds extend laterally in all directions until they thin out or grade into a different sediment type. Matching strata on opposite canyon walls were once continuous.

### Principle of Cross-Cutting Relationships
Any feature (fault, igneous intrusion, erosion surface) that cuts across rocks must be **younger** than the rocks it cuts.

### Principle of Inclusions
Fragments (xenoliths) of one rock enclosed within another indicate the enclosing rock is **younger**; it incorporated pieces of the older rock during emplacement.

## Section 4: Unconformities

An **unconformity** is a gap in the rock record produced by a period of erosion or non-deposition followed by renewed deposition. Three types:

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Angular unconformity** | Tilted/folded rocks overlain by flat-lying strata | All three types occur in Grand Canyon |
| **Disconformity** | Parallel strata on both sides of the gap | |
| **Nonconformity** | Sedimentary strata overlying igneous or metamorphic rock | |

## Section 5: Fossils

**Fossils** are remains or traces of prehistoric life preserved in rock. **Paleontology** is their study.

### Types of Fossil Preservation
- **Permineralization** — mineral-rich groundwater fills porous tissue (bone, wood); "petrified" literally means "turned to stone."
- **Molds and casts** — shell dissolves leaving a void (mold); minerals later fill the void (cast).
- **Carbonization / impressions** — compression squeezes out fluids, leaving a carbon film; effective for leaves and delicate organisms.
- **Amber** — hardened tree resin; effective for insects.
- **Trace fossils** — indirect evidence: tracks, burrows, coprolites (fossil feces), gastroliths (stomach stones).

### Conditions Favoring Preservation
Rapid burial and possession of hard parts (shell, bone, wood).

## Section 6: Principle of Faunal Succession and Correlation

- **Principle of Faunal Succession** (William Smith): fossils occur in a definite, recognizable order; each time period has a characteristic fossil assemblage.
- **Index fossils** — widespread geographically, restricted to a short time span (e.g., trilobites, ammonites, foraminifera); used to date rocks by comparison.
- **Fossil assemblages** — when no single index fossil is present, the combination of species identifies the age.
- Fossils also serve as **environmental indicators**: coral indicates warm, shallow water; certain shells indicate ancient shoreline positions.

## Section 7: Radiometric (Absolute) Dating

### Atomic Structure Review
- Nucleus contains protons (defines element) and neutrons; orbited by electrons.
- **Isotopes** have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons → different atomic mass.

### Types of Nuclear Decay
| Decay Type | What Happens | Effect on Nucleus |
|------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Alpha decay** | Ejects α particle (2 protons + 2 neutrons) | Mass −4, atomic number −2 |
| **Beta decay** | Ejects β particle (electron) from nucleus | Mass unchanged, atomic number +1 |
| **Electron capture** | Electron absorbed into nucleus | Mass unchanged, atomic number −1 |

### Half-Life and Radiometric Dating
- **Half-life**: time for half of the parent isotope to decay to the daughter isotope.
- After each half-life, half the remaining parent converts to daughter.
- Ratio of parent to daughter isotopes reveals how many half-lives have elapsed → age of sample.

### Common Isotope Pairs

| Parent | Daughter | Half-Life |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| Uranium-238 | Lead-206 | 4.5 billion yr |
| Uranium-235 | Lead-207 | 704 million yr |
| Thorium-232 | Lead-208 | 14.1 billion yr |
| Rubidium-87 | Strontium-87 | 47.0 billion yr |
| Potassium-40 | Argon-40 | 1.3 billion yr |

### Zircon Geochronology
Zircon crystals (found in felsic igneous rocks like granite) incorporate U-238 and U-235 but exclude lead during crystallization — making them ideal radiometric clocks. High melting temperature and hardness make them resistant to resetting.

### Sources of Error
The system must remain **closed**: no external gain or loss of parent or daughter isotopes. Fresh, unweathered rock is ideal. Uranium-238 decays in 13 steps before reaching stable Pb-206.

### Age of Earth
- Earth: ~**4.56 billion years** old.
- Oldest rocks: metamorphic rocks, **4.013 billion years**.
- Oldest mineral: zircon crystals, **4.4 billion years**.
- Evidence from meteorites and moon rocks confirms the 4.56 Ga estimate.

## Section 8: Alternative Dating Methods

### Carbon-14 Dating
- ¹⁴C is produced in the upper atmosphere by cosmic-ray bombardment of ¹⁴N; absorbed by plants via photosynthesis, enters food chain.
- Half-life of ¹⁴C = **5,730 years** → useful only for organic material up to ~70,000 years old.

### Non-Isotopic Numerical Methods
- **Growth rings** — annual layers in tree trunks or shells (dendrochronology).
- **Rhythmic layering** — annual varves in lake sediments or ice cores.
- **Growth lines** in shells.

### Dating Sedimentary Rocks
Sedimentary rocks cannot usually be dated directly by radiometric means; ages are bracketed by dating igneous intrusions above and below the sedimentary sequence.
