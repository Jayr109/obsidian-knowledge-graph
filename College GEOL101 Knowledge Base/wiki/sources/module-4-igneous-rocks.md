Module 4 covers magma formation and properties, igneous rock compositions, igneous textures, classification and naming of igneous rocks, the origin and evolution of magmas, and intrusive igneous structures (plutons). Instructor: Dr. Brandi Lenz.

## Topics Covered

### What is Magma?
- **Magma**: completely or partly molten rock at depth in Earth.
- **Lava**: magma that has erupted onto Earth's surface.
- Three components of magma:
  1. **Melt** (liquid portion)
  2. **Crystals** (silicate minerals, if any)
  3. **Volatiles** (dissolved gases — H₂O, CO₂, SO₂)
- **Igneous rocks**: created when magma solidifies; composed mainly of silicate minerals.
- **Intrusive (plutonic)**: crystallizes at depth; observed at surface after uplift and erosion.
- **Extrusive (volcanic)**: solidifies from lava or volcanic debris at the surface.

### Crystallization of Magma
- As magma cools, Si and O link to form silicon–oxygen tetrahedra first.
- Tetrahedra join with other ions to form crystal nuclei.
- Early-forming minerals have space to grow → better-developed crystal faces.
- Results in interlocking crystals (Fig 4.2 reference).

### Igneous Compositions
Four groups based on silica (SiO₂) and mineral content:

| Group | SiO₂ % | Color | Density | Key minerals | Example rock |
|-------|---------|-------|---------|--------------|--------------|
| **Felsic (Granitic)** | 66–76% | Light | Low | Quartz, K-feldspar, ~10% dark silicates | Granite |
| **Intermediate (Andesitic)** | 52–66% | Medium gray/"salt & pepper" | Medium | ≥25% dark silicates (amphibole, pyroxene, biotite) | Andesite |
| **Mafic (Basaltic)** | 45–52% | Dark | High | Pyroxene, Ca-plagioclase | Basalt |
| **Ultramafic** | 38–45% | Very dark/green | Very high | Olivine dominant | Peridotite |

- **Felsic** = feldspar + silica.
- **Mafic** = magnesium + ferrum (iron).
- Granitic magma: viscous (thick), erupts at lower temperature (650–1100°C).
- Basaltic magma: fluid, erupts at higher temperature (1050–1250°C).

### Viscosity
- **Viscosity**: internal resistance to flow of a liquid.
- Increasing temperature → decreasing viscosity.
- Increasing silica content → increasing viscosity.
- High viscosity: honey (felsic magma). Low viscosity: water (mafic magma).

### Igneous Rock Textures
Texture = size, shape, and arrangement of mineral grains — reveals cooling environment.

| Texture | Description | Cooling rate | Origin |
|---------|-------------|--------------|--------|
| **Phaneritic** (coarse-grained) | Large visible crystals | Slow | Intrusive (deep) |
| **Aphanitic** (fine-grained) | Microscopic crystals | Fast | Extrusive |
| **Porphyritic** | Large phenocrysts in finer groundmass | Slow then fast | Two-stage cooling |
| **Vesicular** | Voids from gas bubbles | Very fast | Extrusive |
| **Glassy** | No crystals; ions frozen | Extremely fast | Extrusive |
| **Pyroclastic (fragmental)** | Consolidated ejected fragments | Explosive eruption | Extrusive |
| **Pegmatitic** | Exceptionally coarse | Extremely slow (late-stage) | Intrusive |

- **Phenocrysts**: large crystals in porphyritic rock.
- **Groundmass**: fine-grained matrix surrounding phenocrysts.

### Named Igneous Rocks

**Felsic:**
- **Granite**: coarse-grained (phaneritic); 10–20% quartz, ~50% K-feldspar, <10% dark silicates; most common intrusive rock.
- **Rhyolite**: fine-grained (aphanitic) equivalent of granite; buff to pink or light gray.
- **Obsidian**: glassy, dark-colored (metallic ions tint it); chemically equivalent to granite; conchoidal fracture.
- **Pumice**: glassy + vesicular; frothy lava; light enough to float on water.

**Intermediate:**
- **Andesite**: medium-gray, fine-grained; often porphyritic; volcanic origin.
- **Diorite**: intrusive equivalent of andesite; coarse-grained; gray, salt-and-pepper; no visible quartz.

**Mafic:**
- **Basalt**: very dark green to black; fine-grained; pyroxene + Ca-plagioclase; most common extrusive rock; forms oceanic crust, Hawaiian Islands, Iceland.
- **Gabbro**: intrusive equivalent of basalt; coarse-grained; major part of oceanic crust.

**Ultramafic:**
- **Peridotite**: mostly olivine + pyroxene; main constituent of upper mantle; rare at surface.

**Pyroclastic rocks:**
- **Tuff**: most common pyroclastic rock; ash-sized fragments cemented together.
- **Welded tuff**: ash particles fuse together while hot; covers much of the western U.S.
- **Volcanic breccia**: particles larger than ash; includes lava blobs, blocks, ash, glass.

### Origin of Magma
- **Geothermal gradient**: temperature in upper crust increases ~25°C/km.
- Lower crust and upper mantle rocks are near their melting points → tectonic processes trigger melting.

Three triggers:
1. **Decompression melting**: reducing confining pressure lowers melting temperature.
   - Occurs at divergent boundaries (spreading centers) and mantle plume hot spots.
2. **Addition of water**: water (and other volatiles) lower the melting temperature of mantle rocks.
   - Occurs mainly at subduction zones — water driven from subducting oceanic crust migrates into overlying mantle wedge.
3. **Temperature increase**: basaltic magma ponds beneath lower-density continental crust → heats and melts felsic minerals.

### Bowen's Reaction Series
- Minerals crystallize in a systematic order based on melting points.
- **First to crystallize**: olivine (highest melting point).
- As minerals crystallize, melt becomes depleted in Fe, Mg, Ca → residual melt enriched in Si, K, Na.
- After ~1/3 crystallization, remaining melt severely depleted in mafic components.

### How Magmas Evolve
- **Magmatic differentiation**: formation of one or more secondary magmas from a single parent magma.
- **Crystal settling**: earlier-formed denser minerals sink to base of magma chamber → remaining liquid has different composition.
- **Assimilation**: magma ascending fractures rock; dislodged country rock melts into the melt → bulk composition changes.
- **Magma mixing**: two distinct magma bodies merge → blend of composition.

### Partial Melting
- Most magmas produced by **incomplete melting** (partial melting).
- Enriches melt in ions from lowest-melting-point minerals.
- Partial melt of **ultramafic** rock → mafic magma.
- Partial melt of **mafic** rock → intermediate magma.
- Partial melt of **intermediate** rock → felsic magma.
- Most erupted magma is basaltic — originates from partial melting of mantle at oceanic ridges (called **primary/primitive magma**).

### Intrusive Igneous Bodies (Plutons)
A **pluton** = cooled magma emplaced into preexisting rocks.

Classification:
- **Tabular** (table-like):
  - *Discordant* (cuts across structures): **Dike** — injected into fractures cutting across bedding; transports magma upward; parallel dikes = dike swarms.
  - *Concordant* (parallel to strata): **Sill** — horizontal; exploits bedding plane weaknesses; may show **columnar jointing** (6-sided shrinkage columns from cooling).
- **Massive** (blob-shaped):
  - **Batholith**: largest intrusive body; ≥100 km² exposure; typically felsic to intermediate; common along western margins of continents.
  - **Stock**: smaller than batholith (<100 km²).
  - **Laccolith**: forcibly injected between strata; arches overlying strata upward; "overinflated sill."
- **Stoping**: mechanism for batholith emplacement — overlying blocks of country rock sink through magma.
- **Xenoliths**: suspended blocks of country rock found within plutons.
- **Columnar jointing**: shrinkage fractures forming elongated 6-sided pillars (occurs in sills and some lava flows).
