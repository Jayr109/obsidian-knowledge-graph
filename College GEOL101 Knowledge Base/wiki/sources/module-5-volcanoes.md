Module 5 covers the nature of volcanic eruptions, volcano types, lava and pyroclastic materials, the anatomy of volcanoes, tectonic settings, and volcanic hazards (including case studies of Mt. St. Helens and Pompeii). Instructor: Dr. Brandi Lenz.

## Topics Covered

### What is a Volcano?
- A **volcano** is a mountain constructed by the eruption of molten rock from Earth's interior.
- Volcanoes are a direct consequence of **plate tectonics** and **mantle convection**.

### Locations of Volcanic Activity
1. Divergent plate boundaries (especially mid-ocean ridges) — most of Earth's volcanic activity.
2. Convergent plate boundaries (subduction zones) — where most visible surface volcanoes occur.
3. Hot spots.

### Classifying Volcanoes by Activity
- **Active**: currently erupting or erupted recently; likely to erupt again.
- **Dormant**: not erupted recently; may erupt in the future.
- **Extinct**: not expected to erupt; incapable of future eruption.

### Magma/Lava Types

| Type | Source | Viscosity | Temperature | Gas % | SiO₂ % | Explosiveness |
|------|--------|-----------|-------------|-------|---------|---------------|
| Basaltic (mafic) | Upper mantle | Lowest | Highest (~1050–1250°C) | 1–2% | ~50% | Least |
| Andesitic (intermediate) | Ocean crust + sediments | Medium | Medium | 3–4% | ~60% | Medium |
| Rhyolitic (felsic) | Continental crust | Highest | Lowest (650–1100°C) | 4–6% | ~70% | Most |

### Eruption Types
- **Effusive eruptions**: low-viscosity; gas escapes easily; broad lava flows; mafic/ultramafic.
- **Explosive eruptions**: high-viscosity felsic magma; gas trapped → pressure builds → violent release; point-source; associated with subduction.
- **What determines explosiveness?** Magma type (especially silica content) → controls viscosity → controls how easily gas escapes.

### Volcano Landforms

#### Effusive Eruption Landforms
- **Shield volcanoes**: largest in size; gentle slope; quiet non-explosive eruptions; basaltic; mainly lava flows with some tephra. Example: Mauna Loa (largest volcano on Earth), Hawaiian Islands, Canary Islands, Galapagos. Begin as seamounts.
- **Flood basalts (Large Igneous Provinces)**: fluid basaltic lava from fissures blanketing large areas. Examples: Columbia River Plateau, Deccan Traps.

#### Explosive Eruption Landforms
- **Composite volcanoes (stratovolcanoes)**: large, symmetrical cone; thousands of feet high; interbedded andesitic lava flows + pyroclastic layers; many in the Ring of Fire. Examples: Mt. St. Helens, Mt. Rainier, Fujiyama, Mt. Etna, Vesuvius.
- **Cinder cones (scoria cones)**: small (30–300 m tall); steep sides; built from ejected basaltic lava fragments; frequently occur in groups; often on flanks of shield and composite volcanoes (parasitic cones). Example: Paricutín (Mexico).
- **Calderas**: large circular depression (diameter >1 km) formed by collapse of summit after magma chamber drains. Example: Crater Lake (from Mt. Mazama collapse, 7,700 years ago), Yellowstone Caldera.

#### Other Landforms
- **Lava domes**: small, dome-shaped rhyolitic lava mass; thick lava squeezed from vent; collapse → powerful pyroclastic flows.
- **Volcanic necks**: solidified magma in a volcanic conduit exposed after erosion. Example: Shiprock, New Mexico.
- **Pipes**: rare conduit originating in mantle at >150 km depth. Example: Kimberlite pipes (diamond source).

### Anatomy of a Volcano
- **Fissure**: crack in crust as magma moves toward surface.
- **Conduit**: pipe-shaped tunnel carrying magma upward.
- **Vent**: surface opening of conduit.
- **Volcanic cone**: cone-shaped structure from successive eruptions.
- **Crater**: funnel-shaped summit depression; generally <1 km diameter.
- **Caldera**: large circular depression; diameter >1 km; forms by summit collapse.
- **Parasitic cones**: flank vents emitting lava and pyroclastic material.
- **Fumaroles**: flank vents emitting only gases.

### Lava Flows

**Basaltic lava (3 forms):**
- **Pillow lava**: underwater; low viscosity; mid-ocean ridges.
- **Pahoehoe**: on land; lower viscosity; ropy surface texture.
- **Aa lava**: on land; high viscosity; rough, jagged, clinkery surface.

**Andesitic and rhyolitic lava:**
- More viscous than basaltic.
- Short but prominent flows.
- **Block lavas**: upper surface of massive, detached blocks.

**Proportions of lava types:**
- ~90% of lava is basaltic (erupts at oceanic ridges).
- <10% is andesitic (plate margin volcanic chains, island arcs).
- ~1% is rhyolitic (continental settings, with hot gases and ash).

### Pyroclastic Materials (Tephra)
Volcanoes eject pulverized rock and lava fragments; volatiles = 1–8% of magma by weight.

| Material | Size / Description |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Volcanic ash** | Fine glassy fragments; can weld into welded tuff |
| **Lapilli / Cinders** | Walnut-sized material |
| **Blocks** | Hardened/cooled lava >2.5 inches; angular |
| **Bombs** | Ejected as hot/molten lava >2.5 inches; streamlined shape from flight |
| **Pumice** | Light gray/pink porous rock from frothy andesitic/rhyolitic lava |
| **Scoria** | Reddish-brown porous rock from frothy basaltic lava |

### Volcanic Gases
- Volatiles = 1–8% of magma weight; expand as pressure drops at surface.
- Gases: H₂O (most common), CO₂, SO₂, H₂S, CO, N₂.
- CO₂ and SO₂ most toxic.

### Tectonic Setting of Volcanoes

| Setting | Magma type | Eruption style | Volcano type | Examples |
|---------|-----------|----------------|--------------|---------|
| Convergent margins (subduction) | Andesitic | Explosive | Composite | Mt. St. Helens, Vesuvius, Fujiyama |
| Divergent margins (mid-ocean ridges, rift zones) | Basaltic (more felsic at continental rifts) | Quiet | Shield volcanoes | Iceland, mid-ocean ridges |
| Hot spots (oceanic crust) | Basaltic | Quiet | Shield | Hawaiian Islands |
| Hot spots (continental crust) | Rhyolitic | Most explosive; caldera eruptions | Caldera | Yellowstone |

### Volcanic Hazards

**Primary:**
- **Lava flows**: can travel tens of km; speeds up to 19 mph on steep slopes; property destruction (paths predictable).
- **Pyroclastic flows (nuée ardente)**: mixture of hot gases + incandescent ash + lava fragments; gravity-propelled (like snow avalanche); speeds can exceed 100 km/h; generated by collapse of eruption columns.
  - **Surges**: small ash amounts separating from main pyroclastic flow; can knock down buildings.
  - 1902: St. Pierre, Martinique destroyed by pyroclastic flow from Mount Pelée.
  - 79 CE: Pompeii buried by ash + pumice from Vesuvius; pyroclastic flows killed survivors.
- **Volcanic bombs**: ejected molten blobs.
- **Ash deposits**: bury landscapes, kill crops, cause roof collapse (heavy), respiratory hazard, damage jet engines; can reach stratosphere → reflect sunlight → temporary cooling ("volcanic winter"). Mt. Tambora 1815 → "Year Without a Summer."
- **Poisonous gases**: CO₂ and SO₂ most dangerous; water vapor deadly as direct steam contact.

**Secondary effects:**
- **Lahars**: volcanic mudflows; debris saturated with water rapidly moves downslope; triggered by snowmelt from rising magma. 1985 Nevado del Ruiz lahar killed ~25,000 people.
- **Debris flows/avalanches**: >50% coarse debris.
- **Floods, fires**.
- **Contamination of surface water and groundwater**.
- **Acid rain**.
- **Tsunamis**: triggered by volcanic landslides (example: Krakatau collapse).

### Volcanic Landslides
- Common on volcanic cones (tall steep slopes).
- Triggers: magma intrusion, explosive eruptions, large earthquakes, heavy rainfall.
- Can trigger volcanic eruptions (Mt. St. Helens 1980) and tsunamis.

### Case Study: Mt. St. Helens 1980
- Magma chamber inflated as magma rose → bulge on north slope.
- Earthquake weakened north slope → it slipped → pressure on magma chamber released → dissolved gases expanded → lateral blast.
- Multiple lahars generated.
- Ash fall across wide area of Pacific Northwest.
- ~600 homes and structures destroyed.
- NASA photo 2015 shows recovery 35 years later.

### Case Study: Pompeii / Vesuvius (79 CE)
- Mt. Vesuvius: composite volcano.
- Ash and pumice fell at 12–15 cm/hr.
- Pyroclastic flow killed all remaining survivors.

### Why Study Volcanoes?
- >450 million people (~9% of Earth's population) live near active volcanoes.
- Mt. Rainier: 54 miles from Seattle (563,374 people).
- Popocatépetl: 43 miles from Mexico City (9 million people).
- Fujiyama: 62 miles from Tokyo (12 million people).
- ~139 active volcanoes worldwide; 18–20 in highly active state.
- 90% of most dangerous volcanoes in Indonesia, Philippines, Japan, Mexico, Ethiopia.
- Prediction and evacuation are the only tools — eruptions cannot be stopped.

### Monitoring Volcanic Activity
- Changes in earthquake patterns.
- Inflation or deflation of volcanic cone.
- Changes in amount and/or composition of volcanic gases.
