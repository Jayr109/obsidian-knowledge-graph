Module 1 introduces the field of geology, the scientific method, the origin and early evolution of Earth, Earth's internal structure, the rock cycle, and the major surface features of Earth. Instructor: Dr. Brandi Lenz.

## Topics Covered

### What is Geology?
- **Physical geology** — examines Earth's materials and processes at and beneath the surface.
- **Historical geology** — seeks to understand Earth's origin and development through time.
- Why study geology: resource use, environmental health, geologic hazards (e.g., 2010 BP Deepwater Horizon spill, 2011 Tōhoku Earthquake).

### Human Population Growth
- Exponential growth formula: **N = N₀eᵏᵗ** (N = future value, N₀ = present value, e = 2.71828, k = growth rate as decimal, t = years).
- Doubling time: **D = 70 / G** (where G is growth rate as percentage).
- 1962 peak global growth rate: 2.2%, implying ~32-year doubling time. Today ~44 years.

### Earth as a System
- Input-output analysis: method for analyzing change in a system.
- **Uniformitarianism**: "The present is the key to the past" — James Hutton (1785).
- **Environmental Unity**: one action causes a chain of others; "the present is the key to the future."

### The Scientific Method
- Science assumes the natural world behaves consistently and predictably.
- **Hypothesis**: tentative, untested explanation — must fit observations and be testable.
- **Theory**: well-tested, widely accepted explanation of observable facts.
- **Scientific Method**: raise questions → gather data → formulate and test hypotheses.

### Origin of Earth
- Universe began with the **Big Bang**.
- **Nebular theory**: solar system evolved from a rotating cloud (solar nebula) of H, He, and dust.
  - Collapse under gravity → rotating disc → protosun + planets.
  - Inner planets from metallic/rocky material; outer planets from ices.
- Early Earth: iron and nickel melted, sank to center (**chemical differentiation**) → core, mantle, crust.
- Primitive atmosphere from volcanic gases.

### Earth's Internal Structure
- **By composition:**
  - Crust: oceanic (8 km thick, basalt) vs. continental (~45 km, granite).
  - Mantle: thick shell, composition of peridotite (mineral: olivine). Upper (olivine, Si), Lower (Fe, Mg, Si, high-density).
  - Core: metallic iron + nickel. Outer core = liquid; inner core = solid (pressure keeps it solid despite high temperature).
- **By physical properties:**
  - **Lithosphere**: rigid crust + uppermost mantle; thicker under continents. Forms tectonic plates.
  - **Asthenosphere**: ductile/plastic region (100–350 km depth); "silly putty" behavior.
  - **Mesosphere**: solid, high-strength lower mantle (350 km → 2883 km).
- Determined using seismic waves (slide reference — visual diagram).

### Rock Cycle
- Three major rock types: **Igneous**, **Sedimentary**, **Metamorphic**.
- **Igneous**: formed from cooling of magma (intrusive) or lava (extrusive).
- **Sedimentary**: formed by accumulation and cementation of sediments. Types: clastic, biological, chemical.
- **Metamorphic**: formed when rocks are subjected to intense heat and/or pressure. Types: foliated (regional) and non-foliated (contact).

### Face of Earth
- **Ocean floor features:**
  - Continental shelf → continental slope → continental rise.
  - Abyssal plain (flat deep ocean).
  - Deep-ocean trenches (narrow, deep depressions).
  - Seamounts (small volcanic structures).
  - Oceanic ridges (most prominent ocean floor feature; igneous rock, fractured and uplifted).
- **Continental features:**
  - Mountain belts (most prominent).
  - Cratons (stable interiors).
  - Shields (flat regions of deformed crystalline rock within cratons).
  - Stable platforms (flat cratons covered with sedimentary rock veneer).
