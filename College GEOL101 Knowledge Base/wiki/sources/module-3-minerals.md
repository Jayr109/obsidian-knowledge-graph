Module 3 covers the chemistry of minerals (atomic structure, bonding), the definition and physical properties of minerals, mineral groups (silicate and nonsilicate), silicate structures, and mineral formation and destruction. Instructor: Dr. Brandi Lenz.

## Topics Covered

### Why Study Minerals?
- An average person uses 3.5 million pounds of minerals, metals, and fuels in a lifetime.
- Minerals are the building blocks of rocks.

### Definition of a Mineral
Five criteria — all must be met:
1. **Naturally occurring**
2. **Generally inorganic**
3. **Solid**
4. **Definite chemical composition** (allows some variation)
5. **Orderly internal crystalline structure** (repeating 3-D crystal lattice)

Example: Quartz = SiO₂. Ice qualifies (H₂O meets all five criteria).

### Atomic Structure
- **Atoms**: smallest particles of matter that cannot be chemically split.
  - Protons (+1), Neutrons (0), Electrons (−1).
  - Electrons in **principal shells**; outermost shell = **valence electrons** (determine bonding behavior).
- **Atomic number**: number of protons — determines chemical identity.
- **Elements**: ~90 natural; organized in periodic table.
- **Compounds**: most elements join others (e.g., halite = NaCl).

### Types of Chemical Bonding
- **Ionic bonding**: atoms gain/lose valence electrons → ions; oppositely charged ions attract.
  - Example: Halite (NaCl) — Na loses one electron (Na⁺), Cl gains one (Cl⁻).
- **Covalent bonding**: atoms share valence electrons.
  - Example: H₂.
- **Metallic bonding**: valence electrons migrate freely among atoms → high electrical conductivity, malleable (hammered into sheets), ductile (drawn into wires).
- **Van der Waals bonds**: very weak forces between layers.
  - Example: Graphite — same composition (C) as diamond but bonded in sheets with Van der Waals between layers → very soft.
- **Hybrid bonds**: mixture of sharing and transfer — silicate minerals.
- **Octet rule**: atoms tend to gain, lose, or share electrons until surrounded by 8 valence electrons.

### Diamond vs. Graphite (Polymorphs of Carbon)
- Both are pure carbon (C) — **polymorphs** (same composition, different structure).
- Diamond: all covalent bonds in a 3-D framework → extremely hard.
- Graphite: covalent bonds in sheets + Van der Waals between sheets → very soft.

### Physical Properties of Minerals
| Property | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Color** | Most obvious but most misleading (impurities can change it) |
| **Streak** | Color of powdered mineral — consistent regardless of outward color |
| **Hardness** | Resistance to scratching; determined by bond strength; measured on **Mohs Hardness Scale** |
| **Cleavage** | Tendency to break along preferred flat surfaces (planes of weakest bonds); predictable and repeatable |
| **Fracture** | Irregular breaking in minerals without cleavage (e.g., conchoidal fracture in quartz) |
| **Luster** | Way a mineral reflects light (glassy/vitreous, metallic, silky, etc.) |
| **Crystal form** | Geometric arrangement of faces when mineral grows unrestricted; reflects internal structure |
| **Specific gravity** | Ratio of mineral's weight in air to weight in water |
| **Special properties** | Reaction with acids (calcite), magnetism (magnetite), odor |

### The Eight Most Abundant Elements in Continental Crust
Represent >98% by weight: O, Si, Al, Fe, Ca, Na, K, Mg.

### Mineral Groups
**Silicate minerals** (>90% of Earth's crust, >800 known):
- Contain the silicate ion (SiO₄)⁴⁻ tetrahedron as building block.
- Structures from simple to complex: single tetrahedra → single chains → double chains → sheets → 3D frameworks.

**Nonsilicate minerals** (~8% of crust):
- Oxides (O²⁻): hematite (Fe₂O₃), magnetite (Fe₃O₄)
- Sulfides (S²⁻, metallic luster): pyrite (FeS₂), galena (PbS)
- Carbonates (CO₃²⁻): calcite (CaCO₃), dolomite CaMg(CO₃)₂
- Sulfates (SO₄²⁻): gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O)
- Halides (Cl⁻, Br⁻, F⁻): halite (NaCl), fluorite
- Native elements: gold, copper, diamond

### Silicate Structures
- **Silicon–oxygen tetrahedron**: 1 Si⁴⁺ surrounded by 4 O²⁻ — the fundamental building block.
- **Polymerization**: tetrahedra link by sharing oxygen atoms.
  - **Single tetrahedra** (no sharing): olivine, garnet — hard, dense, equidimensional, no cleavage.
  - **Single chains**: pyroxene group.
  - **Double chains**: amphibole group.
  - **Sheet structures**: micas, clays.
  - **3D framework** (all O shared): quartz, feldspar — most common.
- Most silicate structures have a net negative charge (except quartz) → balanced by positive metal ions (Fe²⁺, Mg²⁺, K⁺, Na⁺, Al³⁺, Ca²⁺).

### Common Silicate Minerals

**Light (Nonferromagnesian) Silicates** — light color, specific gravity ~2.7, contain Al/K/Ca/Na:
- **Feldspar group** (most common mineral group, >50% of Earth's crust): two perfect cleavages at 90°.
  - *Orthoclase* (potassium feldspar, KAlSi₃O₈)
  - *Plagioclase* (Na and Ca feldspar)
- **Quartz**: only common silicate of pure SiO₂; very hard; conchoidal fracture; no cleavage; hexagonal crystals; colored by impurities (amethyst = Fe traces, smoky quartz = Al traces).
- **Muscovite** (mica): excellent cleavage in one direction; clear thin sheets.
- **Clay minerals**: fine-grained sheet silicates; product of chemical weathering; make up most soil. Kaolinite used for fine china.

**Dark (Ferromagnesian) Silicates** — contain Fe and/or Mg; dark color; specific gravity 3.2–3.6:
- **Olivine**: single tetrahedra structure; black to green; glassy luster; conchoidal fracture; ~50% of Earth's mantle; common in oceanic crust.
- **Pyroxene group** (augite most common): single chains; black; two cleavages at ~90°; dominant in basalt.
- **Amphibole group** (hornblende most common): double chains; black to dark green; two cleavages at 120°/60° — distinguishes from pyroxene.
- **Biotite**: iron-rich mica; excellent cleavage in one direction (sheet).
- **Garnet**: individual tetrahedra linked by metal ions; glassy; no cleavage; conchoidal fracture; brown-red; 12 diamond-shaped crystal faces.

### Nonsilicate Minerals (Economic Importance)
- Carbonates (calcite, dolomite): limestone/dolostone; road aggregate, building stone, Portland cement.
- Halite: mined for table salt.
- Gypsum: plaster, building materials.
- Hematite/magnetite: iron ore.
- Sulfides (galena → lead, sphalerite → zinc, chalcopyrite → copper).
- Native elements (gold, silver, diamonds).

### Mineral Formation
Three general processes:
1. **Precipitation**: solid falls out of solution (e.g., evaporite salt deposits, geodes).
2. **Crystallization**: like freezing (e.g., magma cooling).
3. **Biological deposition**: organisms transform dissolved material (e.g., coral reefs → CaCO₃).
- Also: **volcanic outgassing** deposits minerals.

### Mineral Destruction
Three general processes:
1. **Melting**
2. **Dissolving**
3. **Chemical reaction**
