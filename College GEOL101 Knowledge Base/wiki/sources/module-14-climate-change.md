Module 14 examines Earth's **climate system** and how and why it changes. It builds from the basics of the atmosphere and the **greenhouse effect**, through the proxy methods used to **detect** past climate, the **natural and human causes** of change, the **feedbacks and models** used to project the future, two well-documented **historical** climate swings, and the wide-ranging **consequences** of present-day global warming.

## Section 1: Climate, Geology, and the Atmosphere
- **Weather** is the state of the atmosphere at a moment; **climate** is the long-term pattern. The **climate system** exchanges energy and moisture among five spheres: **atmosphere, hydrosphere, geosphere, biosphere, and cryosphere** (snow, ice, frozen ground).
- Climate and geology interact both ways: climate drives weathering, flooding, and mass wasting, while plate tectonics, volcanism, and mountain building affect climate.
- The atmosphere is ~78% nitrogen and ~21% oxygen, with climatically critical **variable** gases — **carbon dioxide, water vapor, methane, ozone** — plus **aerosols** (sea salt, dust, smoke, volcanic ash) that seed clouds and block sunlight.
- It is layered by temperature into the **troposphere** (weather; temperature falls with height), **stratosphere** (ozone heating, absorbs UV), **mesosphere** (coldest), and **thermosphere** (hot).

## Section 2: Heating the Atmosphere and the Greenhouse Effect
- Of incoming solar energy, ~**50%** is absorbed at the surface, ~**20%** by the atmosphere/clouds, and ~**30%** is reflected (the **albedo**).
- **Greenhouse effect**: shortwave solar radiation passes through the atmosphere and warms Earth; Earth re-emits **longwave** radiation that greenhouse gases (CO₂, water vapor) absorb and radiate back down — warming the lower atmosphere.

## Section 3: Detecting Climate Change
Proxy records reconstruct past climate (resolution improves toward the present):
- **Glacial ice cores** (trapped CO₂ and oxygen isotopes — e.g., Vostok, Antarctica), **seafloor sediments**, **corals** (¹⁸O/¹⁶O ratio as a paleothermometer), **tree rings**, and **fossil pollen**.

## Section 4: Natural Causes of Climate Change
- **Plate tectonics** — drifting continents and changing ocean circulation (very slow).
- **Orbital (Milankovitch) variations** — eccentricity, obliquity, precession (see [[Ice Ages]]).
- **Volcanic activity** — aerosols and **sulfuric-acid droplets** reflect sunlight and cool climate (Tambora 1815 "Year Without a Summer"; El Chichón 1982 lowered temps ~0.5 °C), though long-term volcanic CO₂ can warm (Cretaceous).
- **Solar variability** — sunspot cycles peak every 11 years, too short to drive long-term trends.

## Section 5: Human Impact on Climate
- **Rising CO₂** from burning fossil fuels and deforestation — documented by the **Keeling Curve** at Mauna Loa (416 ppm in July 2022).
- **Trace gases**: **methane** (~20× more effective per molecule, from agriculture/anaerobic bacteria) and **nitrous oxide** (~150-year lifetime).
- **Aerosols** mostly reflect sunlight (cooling), but **black carbon** (soot) absorbs it and warms.
- **Scientific consensus (IPCC)**: CO₂ and temperature are tightly linked; atmospheric CO₂ is increasing; global temperature is increasing ("unequivocal"); and the warming since the mid-20th century is **"very likely" (>90%) human-caused**.

## Section 6: Predicting Future Climate — Feedbacks and Models
- **Positive feedbacks** amplify change (melting ice lowers albedo → more absorption → more warming; permafrost thaw releases CO₂/methane). **Negative feedbacks** offset it (warming → more evaporation → more reflective clouds).
- **General circulation models (GCMs)** apply physics and chemistry but are imperfect because of system complexity, incomplete understanding, uncertain data (clouds), and spatial averaging.

## Section 7: Historical Climate Change
- **Medieval Warm Period (~900–1300 CE)** — Norse farmed Greenland; the Maya flourished; vineyards grew in Britain.
- **Little Ice Age (~1300–1850 CE)** — advancing Alpine glaciers, frozen rivers (Thames frost fairs), abandoned Greenland colony, famines, the Black Death, French Revolution food riots, and the Irish Potato Famine as it ended. Likely causes: the **Maunder Minimum** (low solar output), increased volcanism, and ocean-circulation changes.

## Section 8: Consequences of Global Warming
- **Sea-level rise** (25 cm since 1870) from **thermal expansion** and **melting land ice**; **Arctic sea-ice** loss and **permafrost thaw** (positive feedback); **ice-shelf collapse** (Larsen B/C); **ocean acidification** (~30% since the 1850s); shifting growing seasons and precipitation; **desertification**; more intense hurricanes, floods, and droughts (record-low Lake Mead); and the looming **sixth mass extinction** (including pollinator and insect declines).
