## Key Themes Across Modules
- **Plate tectonics** as the unifying framework: drives volcanism (Modules 2, 5), controls rock formation (Modules 4, 5), shapes Earth's surface (Module 1), and concentrates earthquakes at boundaries (Module 9).
- **Silica content** controls magma viscosity, eruption style, and rock type (Modules 3, 4, 5).
- **Bowen's Reaction Series** links mineral crystallization order to magma evolution and rock diversity (Module 4).
- **Rock cycle**: igneous → sedimentary → metamorphic → back to magma (Modules 1, 6, 7).
- Evidence for plate tectonics: continental fit, fossil/rock record, magnetic anomalies, hot-spot tracks (Module 2).
- **The hydrologic cycle and surface processes** (Modules 6, 10, 11): weathering supplies sediment; running water and groundwater erode, transport, and deposit it; both are vital, contaminable freshwater resources.
- **External agents of erosion and hazard** (Modules 12, 13): gravity (mass movement), waves and coastal currents, glaciers and ice, and wind each erode, transport, and deposit sediment — and each poses major hazards (landslides, hurricanes, coastal erosion) amplified by human activity and climate change.
- **The climate system** (Module 14): the atmosphere and greenhouse effect tie together the spheres; climate both drives and responds to geologic processes, and human greenhouse-gas emissions are now warming the planet with wide-ranging consequences.
- **Geologic time** underpins the whole course — slow processes over deep time (Module 8).
