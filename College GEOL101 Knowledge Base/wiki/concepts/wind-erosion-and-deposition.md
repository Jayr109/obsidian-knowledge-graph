**Wind (eolian) processes** erode, transport, and deposit sediment, and they are most effective in **arid lands** where the ground is dry, loose, and sparsely vegetated. Wind differs from running water in two key ways: it is far **less dense**, so it can lift only fine particles, and it is **not confined to channels**, so it spreads sediment over broad areas. This page covers how wind moves sediment and the erosional and depositional landforms it creates; the broader desert setting is on [[Deserts]].

## Transporting Sediment by Wind

- **Bed load** — sand grains carried close to the ground (rarely above 0.5–1 m) by **saltation**, bouncing and skipping along the surface.
- **Suspended load** — finer **silt-sized** particles lifted high into the air and carried great distances (Sahara dust reaches the Caribbean). Fine particles are hard to lift until something disturbs the surface.

## Wind Erosion

- **Deflation** — the lifting and removal of loose surface material; it lowers whole surfaces (hard to notice) and scoops out shallow depressions called **blowouts**. During the **Dust Bowl**, deflation lowered vast areas by ~1 m in a few years.
- **Abrasion** — windblown sand sandblasts rock, cutting and polishing **ventifacts** (shaped stones) and streamlined, wind-parallel ridges called **yardangs**.
- **Desert pavement** — as deflation and washing remove fine grains, a tightly packed surface layer of pebbles and cobbles (a **lag deposit** or **stony armor**) is left behind, protecting the ground beneath.

## Wind Deposits

Wind builds two main depositional landforms:

### Dunes (Sand)
Sand accumulates wherever an obstruction blocks the wind, forming a **dune** with an asymmetric profile: a **gently inclined windward slope** and a steeper leeward **slip face** that maintains the **34° angle of repose** for sand. As sand avalanches down the slip face, it builds **cross-bedding** inclined in the wind direction (preserved in [[Sedimentary Rocks]]). Dune types depend on sand supply, vegetation, and wind:

| Dune | Conditions |
|------|-----------|
| **Barchan** | Solitary crescents; limited sand, flat hard ground |
| **Transverse** | Long ridges at right angles to the wind; abundant sand (most coastal dunes) |
| **Barchanoid** | Intermediate between barchan and transverse |
| **Longitudinal** | Ridges parallel to the wind; moderate sand |
| **Parabolic** | U-shaped, opening upwind; vegetation anchors the arms |
| **Star** | Isolated mounds where wind blows from many directions |

### Loess (Silt)
**Loess** is a blanket of windblown **silt** deposited by storms over thousands of years. Its sources are **deserts** (the loess of China comes from central Asian basins) and **glacial outwash** (the loess of the U.S. and Europe is reworked glacial sediment — linking back to [[Glacial Landforms]]).
