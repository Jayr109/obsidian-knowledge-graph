Both surface water and groundwater are vulnerable to **pollution** and **contamination** from human activity. Because clean water is essential and everyone depends on it, water-quality protection is a recurring theme across [[Module 10: Groundwater]] and [[Module 11: Rivers and Streams]]. This page consolidates the sources, types, case studies, cleanup methods, and laws covered in both modules.

## Pollutant vs. Contaminant

- **Pollutant** — a *naturally occurring* substance that harms organisms (acid drainage from ore/coal deposits; harmful algal blooms; viruses and bacteria such as fecal coliform).
- **Contaminant** — a *non-naturally occurring* substance that harms organisms (industrial solvents, gasoline/diesel/jet fuel, pharmaceuticals, acid drainage from mining).

## Point vs. Non-Point Sources

- **Point source** — discrete and confined (e.g., a factory pipe discharging wastewater into a stream).
- **Non-point source** — diffuse (road salt washed into streams, agricultural runoff from fields and feedlots, regional acid mine drainage).

## Surface Water vs. Groundwater Pollution

Groundwater is **harder to clean** than surface water:
- Surface water has **higher flow** (pollutants are diluted and dispersed) and exposure to **sunlight and oxygen** (which degrade pollutants and support aerobic bacteria).
- Groundwater moves slowly with no sunlight; while passing through sand can purify sewage within tens of meters, **coarse gravel and limestone aquifers (secondary porosity)** let contaminants travel far uncleaned (see [[Groundwater]]).

### Common Sources of Groundwater Pollution
Landfill leachate, leaking underground tanks/pipes, septic seepage, acid mine drainage and mine tailings, agricultural seepage and irrigation, urban/industrial runoff, and saltwater intrusion.

## Major Contaminant Types

- **Volatile organic compounds (VOCs)** — TCE (degreaser/solvent), PCE (dry-cleaning fluid), benzene, toluene, xylene; carcinogenic at 1–10 µg/L.
- **Nitrates** — from fertilizer; the most widespread contaminant (found in 49 states).
- **Pharmaceuticals and trace solutes** — estrogen, acetaminophen, caffeine, antidepressants (parts per billion).
- **Metals and metalloids** — including **arsenic** (EPA limit 10 µg/L) and uranium (e.g., contaminated Navajo Nation wells).
- **Pathogens** — *Giardia lamblia*, *Cryptosporidium* (treated with chlorine/ozone or membrane filtration).

## Harmful Algal Blooms (HABs)

Fertilizer runoff (nitrogen, phosphorus) fuels explosive growth of algae, dinoflagellates, and **cyanobacteria** ("blue-green algae"). These deplete oxygen (fish kills) and release toxins like **microcystin**, harmful to wildlife and people.
- **Lake Erie / Toledo Water Crisis (2014)** — a cyanobacteria bloom left **>500,000 residents** without safe tap water for days.
- **Florida red tides** — recurring blooms in South Florida tied to Lake Okeechobee fertilizer runoff and disrupted [[Wetlands]] flow.

## Surface-Water Case Studies

- **Cuyahoga River, Cleveland (1969)** — so polluted it **caught fire**, galvanizing passage of the **Clean Water Act (1970)** and creating momentum for the EPA.
- **Acid mine drainage** — abandoned coal and metal mines contaminate surface and groundwater (Appalachia, Kentucky, Yukon).
- **Martin County, Kentucky** — coal-country tap water contaminated with toxic heavy metals — a rural environmental-justice example.

## Groundwater Case Study: Woburn, Massachusetts

The basis of the book/film *A Civil Action*:
- **TCE and PCE** from five companies (W.R. Grace, Beatrice tannery, Unifirst, New England Plastics, Olympia) contaminated municipal wells G and H.
- **28 childhood leukemia cases and 16 deaths** (1964–1986); W.R. Grace settled for $8M, with ~$70M more for cleanup.
- Now a **Superfund site** remediated with pump-and-treat, UV lamps, hydrogen peroxide, air stripping, and soil vapor extraction.

## Cleanup and Treatment

**Steps**: (1) understand the geology, (2) understand the hydrology (aquifer properties, water-table depth, flow direction/rate), (3) identify contaminants and their sources (determines who pays), (4) choose treatment.

**Treatment methods**:
- **Pump-and-treat** — extract contaminated water and treat by filtration, oxidation, volatilization, hydrogen peroxide, or biological processes.
- **Vapor extraction** — vacuum vapor-phase contaminants, then filter through activated carbon or burn them off.
- **Bioremediation** — inject nutrients/oxygen to encourage aerobic bacteria to consume contaminants (common for hydrocarbons).
- **Permeable treatment bed** — a reactive barrier in the flow path that neutralizes contamination (e.g., an artificial wetland).

> Prevention is far cheaper than cleanup — and **wetlands are nature's own filters**.

## Federal Water Laws

- National Environmental Policy Act (1969)
- **Clean Water Act (1972)**
- CERCLA / "Superfund" (1980)
- RCRA Hazardous and Solid Waste Amendment (1984)
- Water Quality Act (1987)
- Safe Drinking Water Act (1996)
