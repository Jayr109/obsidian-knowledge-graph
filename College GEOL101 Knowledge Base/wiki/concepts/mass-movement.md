**Mass movement** (or **mass wasting**) is the downslope movement of rock, regolith, soil, snow, and ice under the direct influence of **gravity**, with little or no help from a transporting medium like water or ice. It is the geologic process that typically follows [[Weathering and Soil]], delivering loosened material toward streams and the sea; together with running water it sculpts most of Earth's stream valleys. Events range from imperceptibly slow creep to catastrophic, deadly landslides.

## Classifying Mass Movements

Geologists classify events by the **type of material** (bedrock vs. soil/regolith) and the **type of motion** (fall, slide, or flow), with rate generally increasing from creep to avalanche.

| Motion | Description | Examples |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Fall** | Material free-falls through the air from a steep face | **Rock fall** (soil fall is rare) |
| **Slide** | A coherent mass moves on a well-defined surface | **Translational slide** (flat plane); **rotational slide / slump** (curved surface) |
| **Flow** | Material moves as a viscous, often water-saturated fluid | **Creep**, **earthflow**, **debris flow**, **mudflow**, **debris avalanche** |

## Slides: Translational vs. Rotational

- A **translational slide** moves as a relatively coherent block along a flat plane of weakness (a bedding plane, fracture, or foliation).
- A **rotational slide**, or **slump**, breaks away along a curved (spoon-shaped) surface and rotates backward. It leaves a steep **head scarp** at the top and a lumpy, distorted **toe** at the base.

## Flows: From Creep to Avalanche

- **Creep** — the slowest mass movement; soil or rock inches downhill from repeated freeze-thaw or wet-dry cycles. It tilts fences, poles, and tree trunks. **Soil creep** and **rock creep** are the common forms.
- **Earthflow** — a tongue of water-saturated soil/regolith moving downslope.
- **Mudflow** — a fast slurry that is **more than 50% water and mud**; a **lahar** is a volcanic mudflow (e.g., Mt. St. Helens 1980, Mt. Pinatubo 1991 — see [[Volcanic Hazards]]).
- **Debris flow** — fast-moving mass dominated by coarse, gravel- to boulder-sized debris.
- **Debris avalanche** — the fastest; an enormous, very rapid flow of rock and debris (Nevados Huascarán, Peru, 1970, ~175–210 mph, buried ~22,000 people).

Once a mud- or debris flow stops, it sets like concrete, so buried victims must be found within minutes — rescue quickly becomes recovery.

## Related Downslope Hazards

- **Subsidence** — slow or rapid settling of the ground from withdrawal of water/oil/gas or collapse over voids, caves, and mines (forming sinkholes). See [[Groundwater Resources and Hazards]].
- **Snow avalanches** — ~10,000/yr in the U.S.; triggered by heavy snowfall or a weak layer in the snowpack.
- **Underwater landslides** — can generate or amplify **tsunamis** depending on how much sediment moves and how fast.

## Notable Events

- **Vaiont (Vajont) Dam, Italy (1963)** — beds dipping toward the reservoir failed; ~240 million m³ of rock slid in, sending a 230-ft wave over the dam that killed >2,000.
- **La Conchita, California (1995, 2005)** — reactivated earthflow/debris flow destroyed homes and killed 10.
- **Oso, Washington (2014)** — a rain-saturated slope failed across a river, killing 43.
