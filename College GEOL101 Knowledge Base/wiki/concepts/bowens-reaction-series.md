**Bowen's Reaction Series** describes the systematic order in which minerals crystallize from a cooling [[Magma]], based on their melting points. It explains why a single parent magma can produce a wide variety of [[Igneous Rocks]] — the key to magmatic differentiation.

## The Crystallization Order

As magma cools, minerals do not all solidify at once. They crystallize in sequence:
- **Olivine** crystallizes **first** (highest melting point).
- **Quartz** crystallizes **last** (lowest melting point).
- In between, the series runs through pyroxene, amphibole, biotite (the **discontinuous branch** of dark, ferromagnesian silicates) alongside the **continuous branch** of plagioclase feldspar (Ca-rich → Na-rich), converging on potassium feldspar, muscovite, and finally quartz.

## Fractional Crystallization

- As each mineral crystallizes, it **removes** its constituent elements from the melt.
- Early minerals take up Fe, Mg, and Ca, so the **residual melt becomes progressively enriched in Si, K, and Na**.
- After only about **one-third** of the magma has crystallized, the remaining melt is severely depleted in mafic components — it has evolved toward a more felsic composition.

This continuous change in melt chemistry is **fractional crystallization**, the engine behind producing felsic magmas from mafic parents.

## Magmatic Differentiation

Bowen's series underpins the ways magmas evolve:
- **Crystal settling** — early, denser crystals (olivine, pyroxene) sink to the magma chamber floor, leaving a chemically different liquid.
- **Magmatic differentiation** — the formation of one or more secondary magmas from a single parent magma.

It also mirrors **partial melting** in reverse: melting an ultramafic rock yields mafic magma, melting mafic yields intermediate, and melting intermediate yields felsic.
