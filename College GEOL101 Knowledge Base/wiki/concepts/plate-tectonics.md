Plate tectonics is the unifying theory of geology explaining how the lithosphere is broken into rigid plates that move over the asthenosphere, producing earthquakes, volcanoes, mountains, and ocean basins at their boundaries.

## Foundations

- **Alfred Wegener** (1915) proposed **continental drift** — supercontinent **Pangaea** existed ~200 Ma and fragmented.
  - Evidence: continental fit, matching fossils, rock records, mountain ranges, glacial distributions.
  - Rejected at the time: no identified mechanism.
- Post-WWII seafloor exploration → **Harry Hess** proposed **seafloor spreading** (new crust created at mid-ocean ridges).
- **Paleomagnetism** confirmed symmetric magnetic striping parallel to ridges → the "paleomagnetic tape recorder."
- These discoveries led to the modern **theory of plate tectonics** (late 1960s).

## Core Principles
- Lithosphere (crust + upper mantle) broken into **lithospheric plates**.
- Plates "float" on the weak, hot **asthenosphere**.
- Plates move ~3 cm/yr relative to each other.
- Continental lithosphere: thicker, less dense.
- Oceanic lithosphere: thinner, denser — only oceanic lithosphere can normally subduct.
- New oceanic lithosphere created at mid-ocean ridges; destroyed at subduction zones.

## Plate Boundary Types

### Divergent Boundaries
- Plates move apart; new ocean floor generated (**seafloor spreading**).
- Associated with oceanic ridges (>70,000 km long total).
- Rift valley at ridge crest; spreading rates average 5 cm/yr.
- **Continental rifting**: divergence within continent → elongated depression → narrow sea → new ocean basin.
- Examples: Mid-Atlantic Ridge, East Pacific Rise, East African Rift.

### Convergent Boundaries
- Plates move together. Three subtypes:
  - **Oceanic–Continental**: oceanic slab subducts; water-induced partial melting → continental volcanic arc (e.g., Andes, Cascades).
  - **Oceanic–Oceanic**: one slab descends; volcanic island arc forms (e.g., Aleutian Islands, Mariana Islands); deep trench.
  - **Continental–Continental**: neither subducts; collision → mountain belt (e.g., Himalayas, Alps, Appalachians).
- Deep-ocean trenches: Peru-Chile, Mariana, Tonga.
- Subduction triggers partial melting at ~100 km depth.

### Transform Boundaries
- Plates slide past each other horizontally — no crust created or destroyed.
- Most on seafloor (fracture zones joining spreading centers).
- Continental examples: San Andreas Fault (CA), Alpine Fault (New Zealand).

## Driving Forces
- **Slab-pull**: cold, dense subducting oceanic lithosphere pulls the plate.
- **Ridge-push**: elevated lithosphere at ridges slides down due to gravity.
- **Mantle convection**: Earth's internal heat drives convective flow; plates and mantle convection form a single coupled system.

## Evidence
- Continental fit (jigsaw puzzle of coastlines).
- Matching fossils and rock records across continents.
- Matching mountain ranges (e.g., N. Atlantic).
- Paleomagnetism and apparent polar wander.
- Marine magnetic anomalies (symmetric stripes).
- Ocean drilling (sediment thickness patterns).
- Hot spot tracks (Hawaiian Islands, Yellowstone).
