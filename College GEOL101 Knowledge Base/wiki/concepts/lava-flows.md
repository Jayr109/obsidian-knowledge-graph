**Lava flows** are streams of molten rock that erupt onto the surface and solidify. Their form and behavior are governed by **viscosity** — itself controlled by silica content and temperature — making lava flows a direct surface expression of [[Magma]] chemistry and a defining feature of effusive [[Volcanoes]].

## Basaltic Lava (Three Forms)

Low-silica, low-viscosity basaltic lava is the most fluid and produces three characteristic forms:
- **Pillow lava** — erupted **underwater** (e.g., mid-ocean ridges); lava chills into stacked pillow-shaped lobes.
- **Pahoehoe** — on land; **lower** viscosity; smooth, billowy, **ropy** surface texture.
- **Aa** — on land; **higher** viscosity; rough, jagged, **clinkery** surface.

## Andesitic and Rhyolitic Lava

- More **viscous** than basaltic lava, so flows are **short but thick** and prominent.
- **Block lavas** — surfaces of massive, detached angular blocks.

## Proportions of Lava Types

- **~90%** of erupted lava is **basaltic** (mostly at oceanic ridges).
- **<10%** is **andesitic** (plate-margin volcanic chains and island arcs).
- **~1%** is **rhyolitic** (continental settings, accompanied by hot gases and ash).

## As a Hazard

Lava flows can travel tens of kilometers and reach speeds up to ~19 mph on steep slopes. They destroy everything in their path, but because their routes are largely predictable they cause far fewer deaths than explosive [[Volcanic Hazards]] like pyroclastic flows. The fragmental, airborne counterpart to lava flows is [[Pyroclastic Materials]].
