A volcano is a mountain constructed by eruption of molten rock from Earth's interior — a direct consequence of plate tectonics and mantle convection. Over 450 million people (~9% of world population) live near active volcanoes.

## Locations
1. Divergent plate boundaries (mid-ocean ridges) — most of Earth's volcanic activity by volume.
2. Convergent plate boundaries (above subduction zones) — most visible surface volcanoes.
3. Hot spots (over mantle plumes).

## Activity Classification
- **Active**: currently erupting or erupted recently; likely to erupt again.
- **Dormant**: not recently active; may erupt in future.
- **Extinct**: no future eruption expected.

## Volcano Types

### Shield Volcanoes
- Broad, gently sloping; built from fluid basaltic lava flows.
- Quiet, non-explosive eruptions.
- Largest volcanoes by volume (Mauna Loa = largest on Earth).
- Begin as seamounts; grow into volcanic islands.
- Examples: Hawaiian Islands, Galapagos, Canary Islands.
- Tectonic setting: hot spots (oceanic) and divergent boundaries.

### Composite Volcanoes (Stratovolcanoes)
- Large, symmetrical, steep-sided cone; thousands of feet high.
- Interbedded andesitic lava flows + pyroclastic layers.
- Most explosive and dangerous.
- Common in the **Ring of Fire** (subduction zones).
- Examples: Mt. St. Helens, Mt. Rainier, Fujiyama, Mt. Etna, Vesuvius, Popocatépetl.

### Cinder Cones (Scoria Cones)
- Small (30–300 m), steep-sided.
- Built from ejected basaltic lava fragments (lapilli/cinders).
- Often on flanks of shield and composite volcanoes (parasitic cones).
- Frequently occur in groups.
- Example: Paricutín, Mexico.

### Calderas
- Large circular depression (diameter >1 km) formed by summit collapse after magma chamber empties.
- Most violent volcanic features.
- Example: Crater Lake, Oregon (from Mt. Mazama collapse, 7,700 years ago); Yellowstone Caldera (continental hot spot, rhyolitic).

### Other Landforms
- **Flood basalts**: large igneous provinces from fissure eruptions (Columbia River Plateau, Deccan Traps).
- **Lava domes**: rhyolitic; small dome from thick lava squeezed from vent; dome collapse → pyroclastic flows.
- **Volcanic necks**: solidified conduit exposed by erosion (example: Shiprock, NM).
- **Kimberlite pipes**: rare mantle conduits originating >150 km depth; source of diamonds.

## Anatomy of a Volcano
- **Fissure** → **Conduit** → **Vent** → **Cone**.
- **Crater**: summit depression <1 km diameter.
- **Caldera**: summit depression >1 km; from collapse.
- **Parasitic cones**: flank vents with lava and tephra.
- **Fumaroles**: flank vents emitting only gases.

## Eruption Types and Controls
- **Effusive**: low-viscosity basaltic magma; gas escapes easily; broad lava flows.
- **Explosive**: high-viscosity felsic/andesitic magma; gas trapped → pressure buildup → violent blast.
- Key determinant: **silica content** → controls viscosity → controls gas escape.

## Lava Types
- **Pillow lava**: underwater eruption; mid-ocean ridges.
- **Pahoehoe**: on land; smooth/ropy; lower viscosity.
- **Aa**: on land; rough, jagged; higher viscosity.
- **Block lava**: massive detached blocks; andesitic/rhyolitic.

## Tectonic Setting Summary

| Setting | Magma | Style | Volcano type |
|---------|-------|-------|--------------|
| Subduction (convergent) | Andesitic | Explosive | Composite |
| Mid-ocean ridges (divergent) | Basaltic | Quiet | Shield / fissure |
| Oceanic hot spots | Basaltic | Quiet | Shield |
| Continental hot spots | Rhyolitic | Most explosive | Caldera |

## Monitoring
- Earthquake pattern changes.
- Inflation/deflation of volcanic cone (tiltmeters).
- Changes in volcanic gas amount and composition.
