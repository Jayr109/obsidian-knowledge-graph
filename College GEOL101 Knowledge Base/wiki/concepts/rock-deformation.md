Rock deformation refers to changes in the shape, volume, or position of rocks caused by tectonic forces. It occurs primarily along plate boundaries and produces geologic structures — folds, faults, and joints — that record a rock's tectonic history. The study of these structures is called **structural geology**.

## Stress

**Stress** = force per unit area exerted on rocks.

| Stress Type | Description | Associated Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Confining pressure | Equal in all directions; compacts but does not deform | — |
| Compressional | Squeezes/shortens rock | Convergent |
| Tensional | Pulls rock apart | Divergent |
| Shear | Slides one block past another | Transform |

Confining pressure alone does not cause deformation; **differential stress** (unequal forces) is required.

## Strain

**Strain** = deformation induced by stress.

| Strain Type | Description | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Elastic | Temporary; rock returns to original shape when stress removed | Low stress |
| Brittle | Permanent breaks (faults, joints) | Near-surface; cold, low pressure |
| Ductile | Permanent change in shape without breaking (folds) | Deep; hot, high pressure |

### Factors Controlling Deformation Type

1. **Temperature**: high T → ductile; low T (near surface) → brittle.
2. **Confining pressure**: higher pressure → rock more likely to bend than fracture.
3. **Rock type**: igneous and some metamorphic rocks tend brittle; weakly cemented sedimentary and foliated metamorphic rocks tend ductile; glacial ice deforms easily.
4. **Time**: given millions of years, rocks accommodate tens of kilometers of strain by ductile flow.

## Faults — Brittle Deformation

A **fault** is a fracture in crustal rock where displacement has occurred on one or both sides. Faults = result of brittle deformation.

### Terminology
- **Hanging wall**: block above the fault plane.
- **Footwall**: block below the fault plane.
- **Fault scarp**: low cliff formed by vertical surface displacement.
- **Slickensides**: polished, striated surfaces on fault planes from block sliding.
- **Joints**: fractures with no appreciable displacement; most common rock structure; appear in parallel groups.

### Types of Faults

| Fault | Motion | Stress | Plate Boundary | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | Hanging wall moves DOWN | Tension | Divergent | Extension; **Horst and Graben** structures |
| Reverse | Hanging wall moves UP | Compression | Convergent | — |
| Thrust | Low-angle reverse | Compression | Convergent | Hanging wall overrides footwall at shallow angle |
| Strike-slip | Horizontal motion parallel to fault plane | Shear | Transform | Right-lateral or left-lateral |
| Oblique-slip | Both strike-slip and dip-slip | Mixed | Mixed | — |

**Horst and Graben**: alternating upthrown (horst) and downdropped (graben) blocks along parallel normal faults — common in extensional settings.

## Folds — Ductile Deformation

**Folds** = wave-like undulations in rock strata formed primarily by compressional stress. Result: lateral shortening + vertical thickening of crust.

### Terminology
- **Hinge line**: imaginary axis each layer bends around (can be horizontal or inclined).
- **Axial plane**: surface connecting all hinge lines.
- **Limbs**: the two sides of a fold.

### Types of Folds

| Type | Description | Age of Rocks at Core |
|---|---|---|
| Anticline | Upfolded arch | **Oldest** in center |
| Syncline | Downfolded trough | **Youngest** in center |
| Symmetrical | Limbs are mirror images | — |
| Asymmetrical | Limbs are not identical | — |
| Overturned (recumbent) | One or both limbs tilted past vertical | — |
| Plunging | Fold axis penetrates the ground surface | — |
| Dome | Broad upwarp; circular/elongated; typically basement-driven | Oldest in center |
| Basin | Downwarped circular feature | Youngest in center |
| Monocline | Steplike fold in otherwise horizontal strata; coupled with underlying faults | — |

**Key memory aid**: Anticlines → Arch → oldest strata at center. Synclines → Sag → youngest at center. Same logic applies to domes (up) and basins (down).

## Mapping Geologic Structures

- **Geologic map**: view of Earth's surface showing location and orientation of outcropping rock units.
- **Block diagram**: 3D view of crust showing structures at surface and underground.
- **Strike and dip**: standard measurements used to describe orientation of tilted rock layers.
