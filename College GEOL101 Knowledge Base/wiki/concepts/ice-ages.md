An **ice age** is a long interval when Earth's climate cools enough for ice sheets to expand far beyond the poles. The most recent — the **Quaternary Ice Age** — has shaped much of the modern Northern Hemisphere landscape and left a record of repeated **glacial and interglacial** swings. This page covers the ice ages, the lasting marks they left on the land, and the leading explanations for why they occur. The ice itself and its landforms are on [[Glaciers]] and [[Glacial Landforms]].

## Glacial and Interglacial Cycles

- Over the past **~2.6 million years**, ice sheets have advanced and retreated roughly **20 times**, in cycles of about **100,000 years**.
- The most recent peak — the **Last Glacial Maximum** — was ~**18,000 years ago**, when ice covered large parts of North America, Europe, and Siberia.
- The Antarctic ice sheet is far older, having formed at least **30 million years ago**.

## Lasting Effects of Ice-Age Glaciers

- **Sea-level change** — so much water was locked in ice that sea level stood ~**100 m lower** at the Last Glacial Maximum; the U.S. Atlantic shoreline lay >100 km east of today's. Melting the remaining ice would raise sea level by tens of meters.
- **Crustal subsidence and rebound** — the weight of ice **downwarps** the crust; after the ice melts, the land slowly **rebounds** (isostatic adjustment).
- **Reshaped drainage and lakes** — glaciation created the **Great Lakes** and New York's **Finger Lakes** (deepened former river valleys) and rerouted rivers such as the upper Missouri/Mississippi.
- **Proglacial lakes** — ice acting as a dam ponded huge lakes (e.g., **Lake Agassiz**, **Lake Missoula**); sudden **ice-dam failures** released catastrophic megafloods.
- **Pluvial lakes** — cooler, wetter conditions in semiarid regions filled basins far from the ice (e.g., **Lake Bonneville**).

## Evidence and Causes

Glacial deposits interpreted through the principle of **uniformitarianism** show glaciers were once far more extensive. **Tillite** — sedimentary rock lithified from glacial till — preserves evidence of much **older ice ages** (such as a Late Paleozoic one). A complete theory of ice ages must explain both the **onset** of glacial conditions and the **alternation** between glacial and interglacial stages:

- **Plate tectonics** — drifting continents change ocean circulation and carry landmasses toward or away from the poles; this operates over **millions of years** and sets the long-term stage.
- **Variations in Earth's orbit (Milankovitch cycles)** — changes in **eccentricity** (orbit shape), **obliquity** (axial tilt), and **precession** (axial wobble) alter the solar radiation Earth receives and pace the glacial/interglacial cycles.
- **Other factors** — changes in atmospheric composition, ocean circulation, and the **reflectivity (albedo)** of Earth's surface.
