Groundwater is a vital but finite resource. Because [[Groundwater]] often moves and recharges far more slowly than humans extract it, heavy use creates lasting problems: depletion of "fossil" aquifers, land **subsidence**, and **saltwater intrusion**. This page covers groundwater as a resource and the hazards of over-using it.

## The Ogallala (High Plains) Aquifer

The premier U.S. case study of groundwater as a resource:

| Property | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| Extent | ~174,000 mi², South Dakota to Texas |
| Type | Unconfined sand-and-gravel aquifer, up to ~1,000 ft thick |
| Storage | ~3.3 billion acre-feet (1 acre-foot = 326,000 gal) — more than Lake Huron |
| Water age | Mostly **"fossil water"** that entered 10,000–15,000 years ago in a wetter glacial climate |
| Recharge | Minimal under today's dry climate; water table typically <100 m deep |

Irrigation from the Ogallala transformed the former **"Great American Desert"** into the **"Grain Belt of the U.S."** But since the 1930s ~11% of its water has been used and the water table has dropped **50–150 ft** (up to ~60 m) in places. Pumping water that does not recharge is called **mining water** — it is **not sustainable**. Deepening wells is costly, and depletion threatens the regional food supply.

## Groundwater Use and Overdraft

- **Agriculture is the largest groundwater user** worldwide; in the U.S. it accounts for ~**69%** of groundwater use (top states: California, Texas, Idaho, Florida, Arkansas).
- **Center-pivot irrigation** (the circular green fields visible from the air) is a major driver of withdrawal.
- **Groundwater overdraft** occurs when extraction outpaces recharge — the core sustainability problem for aquifers like the Ogallala.
- Water is distributed unevenly: e.g., Canada has ~20% of global water supply but 0.5% of population, while China has ~7% of supply and 21% of population. By 2050, projections put 5–7 billion people under water stress.

## Subsidence

**Subsidence** is the slow or rapid settling of the ground surface. Water in pore spaces holds sediment grains apart; when that water is withdrawn, pores collapse, sediment compacts, and the surface drops.
- **San Joaquin Valley, California** — sank tens of feet from decades of groundwater pumping, and subsidence is accelerating.
- Collapse of surface material over voids, caves, or mines forms **sinkholes** (see [[Karst Topography]]).
- The Leaning Tower of Pisa is a classic example of differential settling.

## Saltwater Intrusion

In coastal aquifers, fresh water is less dense than salt water and floats as a **lens** above it.
- Over-pumping fresh water faster than it recharges draws the underlying **salt water up into wells**, contaminating the supply.
- It is primarily a **coastal** problem and can be corrected with **recharge wells/basins** that replenish the freshwater lens.

## Resource Risk: Keystone XL

Because the Ogallala is **unconfined**, it is vulnerable to surface contamination. The proposed **Keystone XL pipeline** (Alberta tar-sands crude) would have crossed a large area of the aquifer and the fragile Nebraska Sand Hills, raising spill-contamination concerns — a link to [[Water Pollution and Contamination]].
