Sedimentary rocks form by the accumulation and cementation (**lithification**) of sediments derived from weathering and erosion, or by precipitation from solution. They cover ~75% of Earth's land surface and are the primary record of Earth's history, past environments, and life.

## Key Definitions
- **Sediment** — loose particles of minerals or rock from weathering/erosion or precipitation.
- **Erosion** — physical removal/transport of sediment by water, wind, ice, or gravity.
- **Deposition** — when transported sediment settles to rest.
- **Lithification** — consolidation of sediments into rock; includes compaction, cementation, and recrystallization.
- **Cement** — solid material that precipitates in pore space and binds grains.
- **Bedding/Stratification** — horizontal layering of sedimentary rock; surfaces between layers are **bedding planes**.
- **Fossils** — any evidence of pre-existing life (whole organisms, tracks, trails, fecal material); used for relative dating and paleoenvironment reconstruction.

## Three Categories

### 1. Clastic Sedimentary Rocks

Formed from fragments of pre-existing rocks transported and deposited by water, wind, ice, or gravity. Chief minerals: clay minerals, quartz, feldspars, micas. Classified by **particle size**.

| Rock | Particle Size | Key Properties |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| **Shale** | Silt + clay (fine) | Most abundant sedimentary rock; **fissility** (splits into thin layers); quiet environments (lakes, deep ocean); crumbles easily; gentle slopes |
| **Mudstone** | Clay (fine) | Breaks into chunks; no fissility |
| **Siltstone** | Silt (fine) | Less clay than shale; grouped with shale |
| **Sandstone** | Sand | Second most abundant; forms in varied environments; quartz dominant |
| **Conglomerate** | Gravel (rounded) | Poorly sorted; rounded clasts; transported far |
| **Breccia** | Gravel (angular) | Poorly sorted; angular clasts; transported short distance |

**Sandstone varieties**: quartz sandstone (>90% quartz), arkose (appreciable feldspar), graywacke (rock fragments + matrix).

**Particle characteristics**:
- *Sorting* — well-sorted (similar sizes) vs. poorly sorted (mixed sizes); reflects depositional energy.
- *Rounding* — rounded = long transport; angular = short transport.
- Rule: grain size **decreases**, rounding **increases**, and sorting **increases** with distance from source: Breccia → Conglomerate → Sandstone → Shale.

### 2. Organic (Biological) Sedimentary Rocks

Formed from carbon-rich remains of organisms.

- **Coal** — from plant remains accumulated in stagnant, oxygen-poor swamps. Four stages:
  1. Accumulation of plant remains
  2. Peat → Lignite (brown coal)
  3. Bituminous coal (soft coal)
  4. Anthracite coal (hard coal, metamorphic end)
- **Coquina** — limestone made of cemented shell fragments.
- **Chalk** — limestone made of hard parts of microscopic marine organisms.
- **Oil Shale** — from algal remains + clay minerals; contains kerogen.

### 3. Chemical Sedimentary Rocks

Formed from material precipitated from solution by inorganic or organic processes.

| Rock | Composition | Origin |
|------|------------|--------|
| **Limestone** | Calcite (CaCO₃) | Biochemical (shells, coral reefs) or inorganic (travertine, oolitic) |
| **Dolostone** | Ca-Mg carbonate | Mg-rich waters altering limestone |
| **Chert** | Microcrystalline quartz | Silica precipitation; varieties: flint, jasper, agate |
| **Rock Salt** | Halite | Evaporite — restricted seaways or salt flats |
| **Rock Gypsum** | Gypsum | Evaporite |

**Limestone types**:
- *Biochemical*: coquina (shell fragments), chalk (microscopic organisms), coral reef limestone.
- *Inorganic*: travertine (cave precipitation when CO₂ lost), oolitic limestone (ooids rolled in shallow supersaturated water).

**Note**: Limestone is Earth's largest carbon reservoir and plays a key role in the carbon cycle (CO₂ ↔ CaCO₃).

## Sedimentary Structures

Structures that record the conditions of deposition; used to interpret Earth's history.

| Structure | Description | Indicates |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Bedding/Strata** | Horizontal layers | Episodic deposition |
| **Cross-bedding** | Inclined layers within beds | Sand dunes, deltas, streams |
| **Graded bedding** | Coarse at base → fine at top | Turbidity currents |
| **Ripple marks** | Small lithified waves | Oscillation (symmetric) = waves; current (asymmetric) = flowing water |
| **Mud cracks** | Polygonal cracks | Alternately wet and dry environment |
| **Fossils** | Preserved life evidence | Age and ancient ecosystem |

## Depositional Environments

A **sedimentary environment** is a geographic setting where sediment accumulates. The environment determines grain size, shape, sorting, and rock type.

| Category | Sub-environment | Characteristics |
|----------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Continental** | Streams | Dominant agent of landscape change |
| | Glacial | Unsorted clay-to-boulder mixtures |
| | Wind (eolian) | Well-sorted, fine-grained |
| **Marine** | Shallow marine (<200 m) | Terrestrial sediments or carbonate-rich muds (near reefs) |
| | Deep marine | Fine sediments; turbidites from turbidity currents |
| **Transitional** | Beaches, tidal flats, deltas, lagoons | Mixed marine/continental; shoreline zone |

**Sedimentary facies** — different sediment types accumulating in adjacent environments simultaneously (e.g., beach sand while offshore mud deposits); transitions are gradual and can be traced laterally.

## Why Study Sedimentary Rocks?
- Fossil fuels (oil, gas, coal, peat) occur in sedimentary sequences.
- Economic minerals: uranium, iron ore, sulfur, gypsum, silica sand, gravel.
- Building stone: limestone, sandstone.
- Fertilizers: phosphates, nitrates.
- Groundwater resources occur in sedimentary aquifers.
- Record Earth's geologic history, ancient climates, and evolution of life.
