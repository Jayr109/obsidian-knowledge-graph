**Nonsilicate minerals** are mineral groups built around anions other than the silicate tetrahedron. Though they make up only about **8% of Earth's crust** (compared with the >90% formed by [[Silicate Minerals]]), they include many of the most economically important minerals — ores, building materials, and gemstones. They are a major subdivision of [[Minerals]].

## Major Groups

| Group | Anion | Examples | Notes |
|-------|-------|----------|-------|
| **Oxides** | O²⁻ | Hematite (Fe₂O₃), magnetite (Fe₃O₄) | Major **iron ores**; magnetite is magnetic |
| **Sulfides** | S²⁻ | Pyrite (FeS₂), galena (PbS) | Metallic luster; high specific gravity; key ore minerals |
| **Carbonates** | (CO₃)²⁻ | Calcite (CaCO₃), dolomite CaMg(CO₃)₂ | React with dilute HCl; build limestone/dolostone |
| **Sulfates** | (SO₄)²⁻ | Gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) | Plaster and building materials |
| **Halides** | Cl⁻, Br⁻, F⁻ | Halite (NaCl), fluorite | Halite mined for table salt |
| **Native elements** | — | Gold, copper, diamond | A single element |

## Economic Importance

- **Carbonates** (calcite, dolomite) → limestone and dolostone: road aggregate, building stone, Portland cement. They are also the dominant minerals in the chemical [[Sedimentary Rocks]] dissolved to form [[Karst Topography]].
- **Halite** → table salt; **gypsum** → plaster and drywall.
- **Oxides** (hematite, magnetite) → iron ore.
- **Sulfides** → metal ores: galena (lead), sphalerite (zinc), chalcopyrite (copper).
- **Native elements** → gold, silver, and diamonds.
