A **transform boundary** is where two lithospheric plates slide horizontally past one another, with no crust created or destroyed. It is the third plate-boundary type in [[Plate Tectonics]], complementing [[Divergent Boundaries]] (which create crust) and [[Convergent Boundaries]] (which destroy it).

## Characteristics

- Plates move **horizontally past** each other — crust is conserved (neither built nor consumed).
- Motion is accommodated by **strike-slip faults** (see [[Rock Deformation]]).
- Most transform boundaries occur on the **seafloor**, as **fracture zones** that offset and connect segments of mid-ocean ridges (spreading centers).
- They generate shallow but sometimes large earthquakes (see [[Earthquakes]]).

## Continental Examples

- **San Andreas Fault** (California) — the Pacific Plate slides past the North American Plate; the source of major California earthquakes.
- **Alpine Fault** (New Zealand).
