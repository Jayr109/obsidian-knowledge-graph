Silicate minerals are the most abundant minerals in Earth's crust (>90%), all built on the silicon–oxygen tetrahedron (SiO₄)⁴⁻. Their enormous variety arises from different ways tetrahedra link together (**polymerization**).

## The Silicon–Oxygen Tetrahedron
- Fundamental building block: 1 Si⁴⁺ at center, surrounded by 4 O²⁻ at corners.
- Tetrahedra link by sharing oxygen ions — this is **polymerization**.
- Most silicate structures carry a net negative charge (except quartz) → balanced by metal cations (Fe²⁺, Mg²⁺, K⁺, Na⁺, Al³⁺, Ca²⁺).
- Covalent Si–O bonds are typically stronger than the ionic bonds of the overall structure → controls cleavage and hardness.

## Five Basic Silicate Structures

| Structure | Oxygens Shared | Examples | Properties |
|-----------|---------------|----------|-----------|
| **Independent tetrahedra** | 0 | Olivine, Garnet | Hard, dense, equidimensional, no cleavage |
| **Single chains** | 2 | Pyroxene group (augite) | Two cleavages at ~90° |
| **Double chains** | 2–3 | Amphibole group (hornblende) | Two cleavages at 60°/120° |
| **Sheet structures** | 3 | Micas (muscovite, biotite), Clays | Excellent cleavage in 1 direction |
| **3D frameworks** | 4 | Quartz, Feldspars | Hardest, most common structure |

## Light (Nonferromagnesian) Silicates
Light color; specific gravity ~2.7; contain Al, K, Ca, Na; lack Fe and Mg.

### Feldspar Group (most common mineral group — >50% of Earth's crust)
- Form under wide range of temperatures and pressures.
- Two perfect cleavages at 90°.
- **Orthoclase** (potassium/K-feldspar): KAlSi₃O₈; pink to white.
- **Plagioclase** (sodium–calcium feldspar): grades from NaAlSi₃O₈ to CaAl₂Si₂O₈; white to gray; fine parallel lines (striations) on cleavage face.

### Quartz
- Only common silicate composed entirely of SiO₂.
- 3D framework → very hard; resists weathering; no cleavage; conchoidal fracture.
- Often hexagonal crystals.
- Colored by impurities: amethyst (Fe) → purple; smoky quartz (Al) → gray.
- Second most abundant mineral in continental crust.

### Muscovite (Mica)
- Sheet structure; excellent cleavage in one direction → thin clear/silver sheets.
- Used as "glass" in the Middle Ages.
- Produces glimmering in beach sand.

### Clay Minerals
- Fine-grained sheet silicates; large percentage of soil.
- Originate from chemical weathering.
- Kaolinite: common clay mineral used for fine china.

## Dark (Ferromagnesian) Silicates
Contain Fe and/or Mg; dark color; specific gravity 3.2–3.6.

### Olivine Group
- Independent tetrahedra; formula (Mg, Fe)₂SiO₄.
- Black to green; glassy luster; conchoidal fracture; small, rounded crystals.
- High-temperature mineral (first to crystallize in Bowen's series).
- ~50% of Earth's mantle; common in oceanic crust.

### Pyroxene Group (augite most common)
- Single chains; augite is dominant mineral in basalt.
- Black; two cleavages at nearly 90°; important in dark igneous rocks.

### Amphibole Group (hornblende most common)
- Double chains.
- Black to dark green; similar appearance to augite.
- **Key distinction from pyroxene**: two cleavages at 60°/120° (vs. ~90° in pyroxene).

### Biotite (iron-rich mica)
- Sheet structure; excellent cleavage in one direction.
- Dark brown to black; iron-rich member of the mica family.

### Garnet
- Independent tetrahedra linked by metallic ions.
- Glassy luster; no cleavage; conchoidal fracture.
- Color usually brown-red; well-developed 12 diamond-shaped crystal faces.
- Used as gemstone and abrasive.
