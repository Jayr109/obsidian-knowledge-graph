The consequences of present-day **global warming** reach across Earth's systems — from rising seas and a thawing Arctic to acidifying oceans, shifting weather extremes, and accelerating biodiversity loss. Warming is **not globally uniform**: temperature rise is smallest in the tropics and greatest toward the poles, and precipitation changes vary by region. This page surveys those consequences; the underlying science is on [[Climate Change]].

## Sea-Level Rise

Sea level has risen ~**25 cm since 1870**, driven by two processes:
- **Thermal expansion** of warming seawater, and
- **Melting of land ice** (mountain glaciers and the Greenland/Antarctic ice sheets — see [[Glaciers]]).

Projections give ~52–98 cm of rise by 2100 (approaching 4 ft if the rate keeps accelerating). Because ~40% of the U.S. population lives in coastal areas, this worsens **coastal flooding**, **king tides**, and **coastal erosion** (see [[Coastal Hazards and Protection]]).

## The Changing Arctic

- **Sea ice** maximum extent has fallen ~2.8% per decade since 1979 and is thinning. Replacing reflective ice with dark ocean drives the **ice-albedo positive feedback**. (Melting *floating* sea ice does **not** raise sea level — but it devastates ecosystems like polar bears.)
- **Permafrost thaw** is another positive feedback: decaying organic matter releases **CO₂ and methane**.
- **Ice-shelf collapse** — the **Larsen B** shelf (Rhode Island-sized) disintegrated in 2002, and **Larsen C** is fracturing. Shelves are already floating, but their loss can **destabilize the grounded glaciers** behind them, which *would* raise sea level.

## Ocean Acidification

As atmospheric CO₂ dissolves into seawater it forms carbonic acid and **lowers ocean pH** — the ocean is ~**30% more acidic since the 1850s**. Acidification makes it harder for **calcite/aragonite-secreting organisms** (corals, shellfish, plankton) to build shells and skeletons, threatening ocean food webs and the ~20% of people who get much of their animal protein from the sea.

## Weather, Water, and Agriculture

- **Stronger storms** — warmer sea-surface temperatures fuel more intense hurricanes that often move slower and drop more rain (Harvey 2017; Patricia 2015; Typhoon Haiyan 2013). See [[Hurricanes]].
- **More flooding and severe weather**, including tornado outbreaks.
- **Droughts and water stress** — prolonged drought in the U.S. West has pushed **Lake Mead** and **Lake Powell** to record lows; **desertification** is spreading (see [[Deserts]]).
- **Agriculture** — growing seasons have lengthened ~2 weeks, but most crop yields are projected to **decline** as warming continues — a dangerous trend against a still-growing population.
- The U.S. now routinely suffers **14–16 billion-dollar weather/climate disasters** per year.

## Biodiversity and the Sixth Mass Extinction

A **mass extinction** is the relatively sudden loss of large numbers of organisms. Earth has had **five** in its history (see [[Geologic Time]]):

| Extinction | Age | Severity |
|------------|-----|----------|
| End-Ordovician | ~440 Ma | ~57% of marine genera |
| End-Devonian | ~365 Ma | ~70% of marine invertebrates |
| **End-Permian** | ~250 Ma | **~95% of species — the largest** |
| End-Triassic | ~210 Ma | ~52% of marine genera |
| End-Cretaceous | ~65 Ma | ~62% of species (dinosaurs; iridium impact) |

Many scientists argue a **sixth mass extinction** is now underway, with estimates of ~30,000 species lost per year (mainly from habitat loss). A vivid warning is **pollinator decline** — honeybees pollinate ~80% of food crops, yet **Colony Collapse Disorder** (since 2006) and a broader insect decline (a 75% drop in flying insects in one German study) threaten the base of food webs.
