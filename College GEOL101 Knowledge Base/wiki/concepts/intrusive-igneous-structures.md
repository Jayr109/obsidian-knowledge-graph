**Intrusive igneous structures**, or **plutons**, are bodies of magma that cooled and crystallized **below the surface**, within preexisting "country rock." They are exposed only after uplift and erosion strip away the overlying rock. Plutons are the deep-seated counterparts of the [[Igneous Rocks]] and feed the [[Volcanoes]] above them.

## Classifying Plutons

Plutons are grouped by **shape** and by their relationship to the surrounding rock layers:
- **Discordant** — cuts **across** existing structures (bedding).
- **Concordant** — runs **parallel** to existing layers.

### Tabular (sheet-like) Bodies
- **Dike** — *discordant*; magma injected into a fracture that cuts across bedding; transports magma upward. Parallel dikes form **dike swarms**.
- **Sill** — *concordant*; magma intruded along a bedding plane (often horizontal). May display **columnar jointing** — 6-sided shrinkage columns formed as the body cools and contracts.

### Massive (blob-like) Bodies
- **Batholith** — the **largest** intrusive body (≥100 km² of exposure); typically felsic to intermediate; common along the western margins of continents (e.g., Sierra Nevada).
- **Stock** — like a batholith but smaller (<100 km² exposure).
- **Laccolith** — *concordant* mass forcibly injected between strata, arching the overlying layers upward (an "overinflated sill").

## Emplacement Concepts

- **Stoping** — a mechanism of batholith emplacement in which blocks of overlying country rock break off and sink into the magma.
- **Xenoliths** — suspended blocks of country rock found within a pluton (a product of assimilation; see [[Magma]]).
- **Columnar jointing** — elongated 6-sided pillars from cooling-contraction fractures, seen in sills and some lava flows.
