A **divergent boundary** (constructive boundary) is where two lithospheric plates move apart and new ocean floor is generated by [[Seafloor Spreading]]. It is one of the three plate-boundary types in [[Plate Tectonics]], alongside [[Convergent Boundaries]] and [[Transform Boundaries]].

## Characteristics

- Two plates move **apart**; magma rises to fill the gap, creating new oceanic crust (**constructive**).
- Most divergent boundaries occur along the **mid-ocean ridges** (the global ridge system is >70,000 km long).
- A **rift valley** runs along the ridge crest; spreading rates average ~5 cm/yr.
- Earthquakes are shallow and low-magnitude; volcanism is basaltic and generally quiet.

## Continental Rifting

Divergence can also begin **within a continent**:
1. Crustal extension produces an elongated **rift valley** (depression).
2. Continued spreading floods the rift to form a **narrow sea**.
3. Eventually a full **ocean basin** opens.

- Example in progress: the **East African Rift** (with Mt. Kilimanjaro).
- Surface expression on a mid-ocean ridge: **Iceland**.
