**Climate** is the long-term pattern of atmospheric conditions, as opposed to day-to-day **weather**. The **climate system** is the complex, interactive exchange of energy and moisture among five spheres — the **atmosphere, hydrosphere, geosphere, biosphere, and cryosphere** — and climate and geology shape each other (climate drives weathering, flooding, and [[Mass Movement|mass wasting]]; tectonics, volcanism, and mountain building alter climate). This page covers how past climate is detected, why climate changes, and how the future is projected; the physical consequences are on [[Global Warming Impacts]].

## Detecting Past Climate

Because thermometers only span ~150 years, scientists reconstruct older climate from **proxies** (resolution sharpens toward the present):
- **Glacial ice cores** — trapped air gives past CO₂; oxygen isotopes give temperature (e.g., the **Vostok** core, Antarctica).
- **Seafloor sediments**, **corals** (the **¹⁸O/¹⁶O** ratio is a paleothermometer), **tree rings**, and **fossil pollen** (vegetation tracks climate).

## Natural Causes

- **Plate tectonics** — drifting continents and reorganized ocean circulation change climate over millions of years.
- **Orbital (Milankovitch) variations** — cycles in Earth's **eccentricity, obliquity, and precession** redistribute solar radiation and pace the ice ages (see [[Ice Ages]]).
- **Volcanic activity** — ash and **sulfuric-acid droplets** in the stratosphere reflect sunlight and **cool** climate (Tambora 1815 caused the "Year Without a Summer"; El Chichón 1982 lowered global temperature ~0.5 °C). Over long spans, volcanic **CO₂** can instead warm climate (the Cretaceous).
- **Solar variability** — sunspot cycles peak every 11 years, too short to drive long-term trends (though prolonged minima like the **Maunder Minimum** are tied to the Little Ice Age — see [[Historical Climate Change]]).

## Human Causes

The most significant recent forcing is the human addition of **greenhouse gases**:
- **Carbon dioxide** — from burning **fossil fuels** and **deforestation**; its steady rise is recorded by the **Keeling Curve** at Mauna Loa (416 ppm in July 2022).
- **Methane** — about **20× more effective** per molecule than CO₂; tied to agriculture and anaerobic bacteria.
- **Nitrous oxide** — persists ~150 years.
- **Aerosols** — most reflect sunlight (cooling), but **black carbon** (soot) absorbs it and warms.

## The Scientific Consensus

Several conclusions are held with very high confidence (per the **IPCC**):
1. CO₂ and temperature are tightly linked (Vostok record).
2. Atmospheric CO₂ is increasing (Mauna Loa).
3. Global temperature is increasing — warming is **"unequivocal."**
4. The warming since the mid-20th century is **"very likely" (>90% probability) human-caused.**

## Feedbacks and Models

- **Positive feedbacks** amplify change — e.g., the **ice-albedo feedback** (melting ice exposes darker ocean, which absorbs more heat) and **permafrost thaw** (releasing CO₂ and methane).
- **Negative feedbacks** offset change — e.g., warming increases evaporation and **cloud cover**, reflecting more sunlight.
- **General circulation models (GCMs)** apply the laws of physics and chemistry to project scenarios, but remain imperfect because of system complexity, incomplete understanding, uncertain inputs (like cloud cover), and the need to average over large areas.
