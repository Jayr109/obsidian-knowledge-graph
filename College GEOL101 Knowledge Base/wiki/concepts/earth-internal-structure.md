Earth is divided into layers by both composition and physical properties. Understanding this structure is foundational to plate tectonics and igneous processes.

## Compositional Layers (from surface to center)

### Crust
- Rocky material less dense than the mantle.
- **Oceanic crust**: ~8 km thick; composed of **basalt**; denser, darker.
- **Continental crust**: ~45 km thick (range 30–70 km); composed of **granite**; less dense, lighter colored; termed *felsic (granitic)*.

### Mantle
- Thick shell of dense rocky material; less dense than core.
- Composition: **peridotite** (dominant mineral: olivine).
- **Upper Mantle**: olivine, Si.
- **Lower Mantle**: Fe, Mg, Si; high-density.

### Core
- Densest layer; composed mainly of metallic iron, some nickel and other elements.
- **Outer Core**: liquid; same composition as inner core. Generates Earth's magnetic field.
- **Inner Core**: solid — pressures are so high iron remains solid despite extreme temperatures.

## Physical Property Layers (from surface to center)

### Lithosphere
- Rigid layer including the crust + uppermost mantle.
- Rocks are cooler, stronger, more rigid than the asthenosphere.
- Strength is a function of temperature and pressure.
- Thicker under continents than under oceans.
- **Tectonic plates** are sections of the lithosphere.

### Asthenosphere
- Region of the mantle where rocks are ductile, have little strength, and are easily deformed.
- Depth: ~100–350 km.
- Behaves plastically — like Silly Putty.
- Lithosphere "floats" on the asthenosphere.

### Mesosphere
- Lower mantle; solid with relatively high strength.
- Depth: ~350 km to core-mantle boundary (2,883 km).

## Crust vs. Lithosphere — Key Distinction
- The **crust** consists only of oceanic and continental crust.
- The **lithosphere** includes both types of crust *plus* the uppermost mantle.

## How We Know
- Earth's internal structure is inferred from **seismic waves** — their speed and behavior change at compositional boundaries, revealing layer positions and states (liquid vs. solid).
