Paleomagnetism is the study of the magnetic properties of rocks to reconstruct past positions of tectonic plates and confirm seafloor spreading. It is one of the key lines of evidence for plate tectonics.

## Earth's Magnetic Field
- **Dipolar** field — like a bar magnet, with north and south poles.
- Field lines flow from pole to pole; extend into space; weaken with distance.
- Create the **magnetosphere** — protective shield around Earth.
- **Magnetic poles** differ from geographic (rotational) poles.
- A compass points to magnetic north, not true north.
- **Declination**: difference between magnetic north and geographic (true) north.
- **Inclination**: tilt of a compass needle from horizontal (tells you latitude).

## Fossil Magnetism
- Iron-rich minerals (e.g., magnetite in basalt) align with Earth's magnetic field when they form (as rock cools below Curie point).
- This alignment is **locked in permanently** — preserves both declination and inclination at the time and place of rock formation.
- Tells us the **latitude and longitude** in which a rock first formed.

## Apparent Polar Wander
- Sample a sequence of rocks of known age; plot the apparent position of the magnetic pole over time.
- **Apparent Polar Wander** path: pole appears to have moved — but it is actually the continent that moved.
- When comparing paths from two continents: paths are similar but rotated; they nearly match when the continents are restored to their original positions → direct evidence of continental motion.

## Magnetic Reversals
- Earth's magnetic field has **reversed polarity** many times throughout geologic history.
- **Normal polarity**: North geographic pole aligns with North magnetic pole (as today).
- **Reversed polarity**: North geographic pole aligns with South magnetic pole.
- Reversals are recorded in cooling basaltic (iron-rich) rocks.

## Marine Magnetic Anomalies
- **Magnetometer**: measures strength of Earth's magnetic field recorded in seafloor rocks.
- At mid-ocean ridges, strips of normal and reversed polarity alternate symmetrically on both sides of the ridge.
- The "**paleomagnetic tape recorder**": as new oceanic crust forms at the ridge, it records the current polarity → creates mirrored stripes.
- Pattern confirmed seafloor spreading and directly supported plate tectonics theory.

## Role in Confirming Plate Tectonics
- Marine magnetic anomalies provide: (1) evidence for seafloor spreading, (2) spreading rate calculations, (3) ages of ocean floor at any point.
- Combined with ocean drilling results (sediment thickness distribution), provides definitive confirmation.
