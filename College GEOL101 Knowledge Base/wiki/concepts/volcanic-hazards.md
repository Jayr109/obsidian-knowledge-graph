Volcanic hazards affect both the immediate vicinity of a volcano and regions hundreds to thousands of kilometers away. Primary hazards result directly from eruption; secondary hazards follow as consequences.

## Primary Hazards

### Lava Flows
- Can travel tens of kilometers from source (especially basaltic lava).
- Speeds up to ~19 mph on steep slopes; usually slower.
- Paths are often predictable — scientists can warn of property loss, but lava cannot be stopped.

### Pyroclastic Flows (Nuée Ardente)
- Hot mixture of gases + incandescent ash + lava fragments.
- Gravity-driven; move like snow avalanches.
- Speeds can exceed 100 km/h (60 mph).
- Generated by collapse of tall eruption columns.
- **Surges**: smaller ash clouds separating from the main flow; can knock down buildings and move automobiles.
- Historical examples:
  - **1902, St. Pierre, Martinique**: town of ~30,000 destroyed by pyroclastic flow from **Mount Pelée**.
  - **79 CE, Pompeii**: buried by ash + pumice from **Mt. Vesuvius** (composite volcano); pyroclastic flow killed surviving residents. Ash fell at 12–15 cm/hr.

### Volcanic Bombs
- Large molten lava fragments (>2.5 in) ejected and shaped in flight.

### Volcanic Ash
- Fine glassy fragments; hazardous in many ways:
  - Respiratory health hazard if inhaled.
  - Heavy: roof collapse.
  - Can disable jet engines (2010 Iceland Eyjafjallajökull eruption stranded hundreds of thousands of travelers).
  - Buries crops and farmland for years.
  - Ejected high into stratosphere → reflects sunlight → **volcanic winter** (Mt. Tambora 1815 → "Year Without a Summer").
  - Impacts can last for years hundreds of miles from source.

### Poisonous Gases
- Volcanoes emit H₂O (steam), CO₂, SO₂, H₂S, CO, N₂.
- **CO₂ and SO₂** most toxic.
- Steam can be deadly as direct contact at high temperatures.

## Secondary Hazards

### Lahars (Volcanic Mudflows)
- Volcanic debris saturated with water; rapidly moves downslope.
- Triggered by: snowmelt when magma nears surface, heavy rainfall, crater lake breach.
- Highly destructive — can travel far down river valleys.
- **1985, Nevado del Ruiz, Colombia**: lahars killed ~25,000 people (one of deadliest volcanic disasters).
- Mt. St. Helens 1980 generated multiple lahars.

### Volcanic Landslides
- Common on steep volcanic cones.
- Triggers: magma intrusion, explosive eruptions, earthquakes, heavy rain.
- Can **trigger eruptions** (Mt. St. Helens: landslide removed confining load on magma chamber → pressure release → eruption).
- Can **trigger tsunamis** (Krakatau: cone collapse → destructive sea waves).

### Floods
- From rapid snowmelt or crater lake overflow.

### Fires
- From lava flows or pyroclastic material igniting vegetation.

### Water Contamination
- Volcanic gases dissolve in surface water and groundwater → acid contamination.

### Acid Rain
- SO₂ emissions combine with atmospheric water → sulfuric acid precipitation.

## Case Studies

### Mt. St. Helens, 1980
- Magma rose → inflated magma chamber → large bulge on north slope.
- Earthquake triggered landslide on north face → sudden decompression → lateral blast.
- Pyroclastic flows, ash fall across Pacific Northwest, multiple lahars.
- ~600 homes and structures destroyed.
- Forests flattened for miles.

### Pompeii / Vesuvius, 79 CE
- Ash + pumice fall rate: 12–15 cm/hr.
- Final pyroclastic surges killed those who sheltered in place.
- City buried and preserved for ~1,700 years.
