Metamorphic rocks form when existing rocks (**protoliths**) are subjected to intense heat and/or pressure, altering their mineralogy, texture, and sometimes chemistry — while remaining essentially solid throughout the process. They are the third major rock type in the [[Rock Cycle]], alongside [[Igneous Rocks]] and [[Sedimentary Rocks]].

## Formation Basics

- Protolith can be any rock type: igneous, sedimentary, or a previous metamorphic rock.
- The rock stays solid; melting produces igneous rock, not metamorphic.
- Changes: mineral size/shape (recrystallization), new minerals (neocrystallization), sometimes new bulk chemistry.

## Metamorphic Processes

### Recrystallization
Minerals change in size and/or shape without new mineral species forming.
- **Pressure solution**: minerals partially dissolve at grain contacts, then recrystallize elsewhere.
- **Plastic deformation**: grains soften under heat and pressure, flow into aligned orientations — creating **foliation**.

### Neocrystallization
Original minerals become unstable and transform into new minerals.
- Can produce **polymorphs**: same chemistry, different crystal structure (e.g., diamond and graphite are both carbon; Al₂SiO₅ exists as kyanite, sillimanite, and andalusite depending on P/T).
- Can change bulk chemistry via ion exchange with fluids (similar to chemical weathering).

## Agents of Metamorphism

| Agent | Role |
|---|---|
| Heat | Most important; provides energy for reactions; sources: geothermal gradient (~25°C/km) and magma contact |
| Confining pressure | Equal in all directions; compacts rock; does not itself cause deformation |
| Differential stress | Unequal forces → drives deformation and foliation development |
| Chemically active fluids | Hot hydrothermal solutions enhance ion migration and recrystallization |

Parent rock composition largely controls outcomes; most metamorphic rocks preserve bulk chemistry except for loss/gain of volatiles (H₂O, CO₂).

## Metamorphic Grade

Degree of change, from **low grade** (low T/P) to **high grade** (high T/P). Textural progression (shale protolith):

```
Shale → Slate → Phyllite → Schist → Gneiss
       (low)                         (high)
```

## Textures

### Foliation
Planar alignment of mineral grains. The most diagnostic metamorphic texture.

| Type | Grade | Visible Minerals? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rock/slaty cleavage | Low | No | Slate |
| Schistosity | Medium–high | Yes (micas) | Schist |
| Gneissic banding | High | Yes (light/dark bands) | Gneiss |

Foliation forms by: rotation of platy minerals, recrystallization, or flattening of grains under differential stress.

### Non-Foliated
Forms where compressional stress is minimal → equidimensional crystal growth.
- **Porphyroblastic texture**: large crystals (**porphyroblasts**) set in fine-grained matrix.

## Common Metamorphic Rocks

### Foliated
| Rock | Protolith | Grade | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slate | Shale/mudstone/siltstone | Low | Very fine-grained; splits into thin slabs; rock cleavage |
| Phyllite | Shale | Low–medium | Platy minerals just below naked-eye; glossy sheen; wavy surfaces |
| Schist | Shale (basalt for some) | Medium–high | Coarse platy minerals (mainly micas); may have porphyroblasts |
| Gneiss | Granite (or high-grade schist) | High | Light/dark banding; less cleavage than slate or schist |

### Non-Foliated
| Rock | Protolith | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| Marble | Limestone / dolostone | Crystalline calcite; Mohs hardness 3; varied colors; weathers in acid rain |
| Quartzite | Quartz sandstone | Fused quartz grains; pure form is white; very hard and resistant |
| Hornfels | Shale / clay-rich rock | Fine-grained; "baked" by intruding magma body; contact metamorphism product |

## Metamorphic Environments

| Environment | Conditions | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Contact (thermal) | Low P, high T | **Aureole** around magma body; produces hornfels |
| Hydrothermal | Chemically active fluids | Mid-ocean ridges (black smokers), hot springs |
| Burial | Confining P + heat | Thick subsiding sedimentary basins |
| Subduction zone | High P before T | Differential stress dominant |
| Regional | High P + T + differential stress | Mountain belts; most abundant metamorphic rock setting |
| Fault zone | Ductile flow at depth | Produces **mylonites** |
| Impact (shock) | Meteoroid impact | Produces **impactites** |
