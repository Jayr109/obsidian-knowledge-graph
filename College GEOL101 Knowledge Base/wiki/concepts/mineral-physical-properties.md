**Physical properties** are the observable characteristics used to identify [[Minerals]] in hand sample. Because each mineral has a definite composition and an orderly internal crystalline structure, it expresses a consistent set of properties — color, streak, hardness, cleavage, luster, and more. These properties are the practical toolkit of mineral identification.

## The Main Properties

| Property | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Color** | The most obvious but most **misleading** property — impurities can change it (e.g., quartz varieties). |
| **Streak** | Color of the **powdered** mineral (rubbed on unglazed porcelain); far more consistent than outward color. |
| **Hardness** | Resistance to scratching; controlled by bond strength. Measured on the **Mohs Hardness Scale** (1 = talc → 10 = diamond). |
| **Cleavage** | Tendency to break along flat planes of **weakest bonds**; predictable and repeatable, described by the number of directions and the angles between them. |
| **Fracture** | Irregular breakage in minerals lacking cleavage (e.g., **conchoidal** = curved, shell-like fracture in quartz). |
| **Luster** | How the mineral reflects light: metallic, glassy (vitreous), silky, pearly, etc. |
| **Crystal form** | Geometric arrangement of faces when a mineral grows unrestricted; reflects the internal lattice. |
| **Specific gravity** | Ratio of the mineral's weight in air to its weight in water (a density measure). |
| **Special properties** | Distinctive behaviors: calcite **effervesces** in dilute HCl, magnetite is **magnetic**, some minerals have a characteristic odor or taste. |

## Why These Properties Exist

Physical properties flow directly from a mineral's **chemical bonding** and **crystal structure**:
- **Hardness** and **cleavage** track bond strength and the geometry of weak planes — for example, graphite is soft and flaky because weak **Van der Waals** bonds separate its carbon sheets, while diamond (same element) is the hardest mineral because of its 3-D covalent framework.
- **Cleavage angles** distinguish look-alike minerals: pyroxene cleaves at ~90°, amphibole at 120°/60° (see [[Silicate Minerals]]).
