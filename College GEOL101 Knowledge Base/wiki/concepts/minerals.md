Minerals are the building blocks of rocks and constitute the solid material of Earth's crust. Understanding minerals requires knowledge of atomic structure, chemical bonding, and physical properties.

## Definition of a Mineral (5 Criteria — ALL required)
1. **Naturally occurring** — not synthetic.
2. **Generally inorganic** — not produced by organisms (exceptions: calcite from coral).
3. **Solid** — must be in solid state.
4. **Definite chemical composition** — specific formula, though slight variation allowed.
5. **Orderly internal crystalline structure** — atoms arranged in a repeating 3-D crystal lattice.

*Test case: Ice (H₂O) — meets all five criteria → qualifies as a mineral.*

## Physical Properties

| Property | Details |
|----------|---------|
| **Color** | Most obvious; also most misleading (impurities alter color) |
| **Streak** | Color of powdered form on unglazed porcelain; consistent regardless of specimen color |
| **Hardness** | Resistance to scratching; controlled by bond strength. Measured on **Mohs Scale** (1=talc → 10=diamond) |
| **Cleavage** | Breaks along flat planes of weakest bonds; predictable, repeatable at any scale. Described by number of directions and angles |
| **Fracture** | Irregular breaking when no cleavage planes exist (e.g., conchoidal = curved, shell-like) |
| **Luster** | How mineral reflects light: metallic, glassy (vitreous), silky, pearly, etc. |
| **Crystal form** | Geometric shape of faces when mineral grows freely; reflects internal lattice |
| **Specific gravity** | Ratio of weight in air to weight in water |
| **Special properties** | Acid reaction (calcite effervesces with HCl), magnetism (magnetite), odor |

## Chemical Bonding in Minerals
- **Ionic**: atoms transfer electrons → oppositely charged ions attract. Example: halite (NaCl).
- **Covalent**: atoms share electrons. Example: diamond (C–C bonds in 3-D framework → extremely hard).
- **Metallic**: electrons migrate freely → conductivity, malleability, ductility.
- **Van der Waals**: weak bonds between layers. Example: graphite (hard within layers, very soft across layers).
- **Hybrid**: combination of sharing and transfer → silicate minerals.

## Mineral Groups
- **Silicates** (>90% of crust): SiO₄ tetrahedron as building block. Most abundant. See [[Silicate Minerals]].
- **Oxides**: O²⁻ anion. Example: hematite (Fe₂O₃), magnetite (Fe₃O₄).
- **Sulfides**: S²⁻; metallic luster; high specific gravity; common ore minerals. Example: pyrite (FeS₂), galena (PbS).
- **Carbonates**: (CO₃)²⁻; react with dilute HCl. Example: calcite (CaCO₃), dolomite.
- **Sulfates**: (SO₄)²⁻. Example: gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O).
- **Halides**: Cl⁻, Br⁻, F⁻. Example: halite (NaCl), fluorite.
- **Native elements**: single element. Example: gold, copper, diamond.

## Formation Processes
1. **Precipitation**: solid falls from solution (e.g., evaporite salt, geodes).
2. **Crystallization**: from cooling melt (e.g., magma → igneous minerals).
3. **Biological deposition**: organisms transform dissolved material (e.g., coral → CaCO₃).
4. **Volcanic outgassing**.

## Destruction Processes
1. **Melting** (back to magma).
2. **Dissolving** (chemical weathering).
3. **Chemical reaction** (alteration to new minerals).

## Economic Importance
- Average person uses 3.5 million pounds of minerals, metals, and fuels in a lifetime.
- ~4,000 named minerals; only ~20 common in rocks.
- "Rock-forming minerals": dozen or so that make up most of Earth's crust.
- "Economic minerals": less abundant but commercially valuable.
