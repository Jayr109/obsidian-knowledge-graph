**Seafloor spreading** is the process — proposed by **Harry Hess** — in which new oceanic crust is generated at mid-ocean ridges and moves away on either side. It supplied the mechanism that [[Continental Drift]] had lacked, becoming a cornerstone of [[Plate Tectonics]].

## Discovery

Post-WWII oceanographic exploration revealed several puzzling facts:
- A continuous **oceanic ridge system** (>70,000 km long) winds through all the major oceans.
- **No oceanic crust is older than ~180 Ma** — far younger than the continents.
- **Sediment on the deep ocean floor is surprisingly thin**, and thinnest near the ridges.

## Hess's Model

**Harry Hess** proposed that new ocean floor is created at the ridges:
- A **rift valley** runs along the crest of each ridge, where magma rises to form new crust.
- The new crust moves outward symmetrically, conveyor-belt style, and is eventually destroyed at subduction zones ([[Convergent Boundaries]]).
- This explains why crust is youngest at the ridge and oldest far away.

## Spreading Rates

- Average spreading rate: **~5 cm/yr**.
- **Mid-Atlantic Ridge**: ~2 cm/yr (slow).
- **East Pacific Rise**: ~15 cm/yr (fast).

## Confirmation

[[Paleomagnetism]] provided decisive proof: **marine magnetic anomalies** form a symmetric striped pattern of normal- and reversed-polarity crust parallel to the ridges — the "paleomagnetic tape recorder" — exactly what seafloor spreading predicts. Ocean drilling (sediment age and thickness increasing away from ridges) confirmed it further.
