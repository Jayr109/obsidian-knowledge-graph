**Orogenesis** is the process that collectively produces a mountain belt; a single episode is called an **orogeny**. Earth's major mountain belts formed at convergent plate boundaries through subduction, continental collision, or terrane accretion. Mountains displaying faulted and folded rocks are called **compressional mountains**. The study of mountain formation connects [[Plate Tectonics]], [[Rock Deformation]], and [[Metamorphic Rocks]].

## Major Mountain Belts

- **Recent (active/young)**: American Cordillera, Alpine–Himalaya chain, western Pacific mountainous terrains.
- **Paleozoic (older, deeply eroded)**: Appalachians (eastern North America), Urals (Russia).

## Types of Mountain Building

### 1. Volcanic Island Arc (Oceanic–Oceanic Subduction)
- Oceanic lithosphere subducts beneath oceanic lithosphere.
- Subducting slab partially melts the overlying mantle wedge → magma rises → **volcanic island arc**.
- Continued growth produces parallel belts of igneous and metamorphic rocks.
- Example: **Japan**.

Subduction zone regions:
- **Volcanic arc**: where magma erupts.
- **Deep-ocean trench**: where lithosphere bends downward; depth related to lithosphere age (old/cold = steep/deep; young/warm = shallow).
- **Forearc region**: between trench and arc; accumulated pyroclastics and sediments.
- **Back-arc region**: opposite side of arc from trench; tensional forces may cause **back-arc spreading**.

### 2. Andean-Type (Oceanic–Continental Subduction)
Oceanic lithosphere subducts beneath a continental plate.

Key features:
- **Volatiles** driven from descending crust trigger partial melting of ultramafic **peridotite** → mafic primary magmas rise.
- Thick continental crust impedes magma ascent → most crystallizes underground as **batholiths** (diorite to granite range). Example: **Sierra Nevada, California**.
- **Accretionary wedge**: chaotic deformed sediments and ocean crust scraped off subducting plate (bulldozer analogy).
- **Forearc basin**: relatively undeformed sediments trapped between accretionary wedge and volcanic arc.
- **Back-arc spreading**: cold subducting plate sinks nearly vertically → trench "rolls back" → overlying plate stretches → back-arc basin can open.
- Example: **Andes Mountains**; California features (Sierra Nevada, Coast Ranges, Great Valley) produced by subduction of the **Farallon Plate**.

### 3. Alpine-Type (Continent–Continent Collision)
When subduction closes an ocean basin entirely, two continents collide.

- Collision zone marked by a **suture** (slivers of oceanic lithosphere; accreted terrane).
- Produces a **fold-and-thrust belt**: thick sequence of deformed sedimentary rocks.

**The Himalayas**:
- India–Asia collision began ~30–50 Ma after Pangaea fragmentation moved India northward.
- India "docked" into Eurasia following oceanic basin closure.
- Tibetan Plateau uplifted; India still moving north.
- Crust shortens/thickens; remainder causes lateral displacement via **escape tectonics**.

**The Appalachians** — built in three orogenies:

| Orogeny | Age | Event |
|---|---|---|
| Taconic | ~450 Ma | Volcanic arc east of North America thrust over continent |
| Acadian | ~350 Ma | Micro-continent collides with North America; thrust faults, metamorphism, granite intrusions; widened N. America |
| Alleghanian | ~250–300 Ma | Africa collides with North America forming Pangaea; material displaced 250 km inland |

Pangaea rifted ~180 Ma; rift formed east of the suture, leaving a piece of Africa welded to North America.

### 4. Cordilleran-Type (Terrane Accretion)
- **Terranes**: distinct accreted blocks of crust (former microcontinents or island arcs) with recognizable rock series.
- Small, dense ocean floor features subduct; large, buoyant features (e.g., low-density andesitic crust) do not — they collide and accrete.
- Subduction continues on the far side of the accreted fragment.
- The **North American Cordillera** assembled from terranes scattered through the eastern Pacific during Farallon Plate subduction after Pangaea breakup.

### 5. Fault-Block Mountains (Continental Rifting)
- Produced by tensional forces during continental rifting.
- Normal faults create alternating horsts and grabens.
- Example: **Teton Range, Wyoming**.

**Basin and Range Province**:
- Largest region of fault-block mountains on Earth.
- Extends ~3000 km N–S; encompasses all of Nevada and portions of surrounding states.
- Two competing formation theories:
  1. After Farallon Plate subduction, northwest movement of Pacific plate generated tension.
  2. ~20 Ma, lower lithospheric mantle **delaminated** from crust → hot mantle upwelled → lateral spreading → crustal tension.

## Connecting Mountain Building to Other Processes

- **Metamorphism**: Regional metamorphism is the dominant environment in collision and subduction zones.
- **Igneous activity**: Batholiths, volcanic arcs, and intrusions are integral to orogenesis.
- **Erosion**: Older mountains (Appalachians, Urals) are deeply eroded and topographically subdued compared to young ranges (Himalayas, Andes).
