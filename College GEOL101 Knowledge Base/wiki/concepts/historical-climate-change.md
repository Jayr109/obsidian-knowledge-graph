Climate has **fluctuated throughout recorded human history**, and the more recent the record, the more detailed it becomes. Two well-documented swings of the last millennium — the **Medieval Warm Period** and the **Little Ice Age** — show how even modest climate shifts (far smaller than the warming now projected) reshaped agriculture, population, and the course of civilizations. They are a key part of [[Climate Change]].

## The Medieval Warm Period (~900–1300 CE)

A relatively warm interval that favored agriculture and population growth:
- The **Norse settled and farmed Greenland** (Erik the Red, Leif Eriksson), supported by a cod fishery.
- The **Maya** civilization flourished in the Americas.
- **Vineyards** grew in the British Isles, and cereal crops thrived.
- Worldwide population grew — but most people lived on the edge, so when the climate later deteriorated, the swollen population was vulnerable to collapse.

## The Little Ice Age (~1300–1850 CE)

When the Medieval Warm Period ended, colder, wetter conditions set in with sweeping consequences:
- **Glaciers advanced** in the Alps, and rivers that normally stayed open **froze** — the Thames hosted "frost fairs" (1607–1814), and ice shaped events like Washington's crossing of the Delaware.
- The **Greenland Norse colony was abandoned**.
- **Famine and disease** spread: top-heavy cereal crops failed in heavy rain and wind; the **Black Death** (1347–1350) killed 25–50% of Europe; **St. Anthony's Fire** (ergot poisoning from a fungus that thrived in the cool, wet climate) caused convulsions and gangrene.
- Social upheaval followed — witch trials, the food riots preceding the **French Revolution** (after 1789 crop failures), and the **Thirty Years War** (1618–1648).
- Adaptation came partly from new crops — the **potato**, introduced from South America, resisted the cold and grew underground (safe from war and weather).
- As the Little Ice Age **ended**, warm, wet conditions let the fungus *Phytophthora infestans* trigger the **Irish Potato Famine** (1845–1850), killing over 1.5 million and driving mass emigration to North America.

## What Caused the Little Ice Age?

Likely a **combination** of factors:
- **Lower solar output** — the **Maunder Minimum**, a prolonged sunspot minimum (~0.5% drop).
- **Increased volcanism** — sulfate aerosols from many major eruptions reflected sunlight and cooled the climate.
- **Changes in ocean circulation** — disruption of the North Atlantic Current and Gulf Stream cooled Europe.

It **ended** as solar output recovered, eruptions waned, industrialization rose, and ocean circulation shifted again.

## The Takeaway

All of these upheavals were driven by climate changes **much smaller** than the warming Earth may soon experience — a reminder that climate change has always mattered to every inhabitant of the planet.
