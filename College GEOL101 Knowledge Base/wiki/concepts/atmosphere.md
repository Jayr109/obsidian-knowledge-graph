The **atmosphere** is the thin envelope of gases that surrounds Earth, behaves like a fluid, and gradually thins out with altitude (it has a surface but no sharp "top"). It is the stage on which weather and [[Climate Change|climate]] play out, and understanding its composition, structure, and how it traps solar heat is the foundation for understanding global warming.

## Composition

- **Permanent gases** (nearly constant): **nitrogen (~78%)**, **oxygen (~21%)**, and **argon (~1%)**.
- **Variable gases** (small but climatically critical): **carbon dioxide**, **water vapor** (0–4%, the source of clouds and precipitation), **methane**, and **ozone (O₃)** — a thin stratospheric layer that absorbs harmful **ultraviolet** radiation.
- **Aerosols** — tiny solid and liquid particles (sea salt, fine soil, smoke and soot, pollen, volcanic ash and dust). They can attract moisture to **seed clouds** and can **block sunlight** from reaching the surface.

The variable gases matter far out of proportion to their abundance because several of them are **greenhouse gases**.

## Vertical Structure

Air **pressure** is the weight of the overlying air, so it decreases with height (average sea-level pressure is ~1013 mb). The atmosphere is divided into four layers based on how **temperature** changes with height:

| Layer | Temperature trend | Notes |
|-------|------------------|-------|
| **Troposphere** | Decreases with height | Holds most weather; ~11 km thick; capped by the **tropopause** |
| **Stratosphere** | Increases (inversion) | **Ozone** absorbs UV and heats this layer |
| **Mesosphere** | Decreases | Coldest layer (to about −130 °F near the top) |
| **Thermosphere** | Increases | "Hot layer"; sparse molecules absorb solar energy |

## Heating the Atmosphere

The Sun emits **electromagnetic radiation**. A few basic radiation laws govern the energy exchange: all objects emit radiant energy; hotter objects emit more total energy and at **shorter wavelengths**; and good absorbers are also good emitters.

Of the Sun's incoming energy:
- ~**50%** passes through and is **absorbed at Earth's surface**.
- ~**20%** is absorbed by clouds and atmospheric gases.
- ~**30%** is reflected back to space — the **albedo** (by clouds, the atmosphere, snow, and ice).

## The Greenhouse Effect

The **greenhouse effect** is the selective absorption and re-radiation of heat that keeps Earth warm:
1. **Shortwave** solar radiation passes through the atmosphere and heats the surface.
2. Earth radiates that energy back as **longwave** radiation.
3. **Greenhouse gases** — especially **carbon dioxide** and **water vapor** — absorb the longwave radiation and re-emit it in all directions, including back toward the surface, warming the lower atmosphere.

This natural effect makes Earth habitable; adding greenhouse gases **enhances** it and drives global warming (see [[Climate Change]]).
