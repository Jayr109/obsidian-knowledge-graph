Geologic time is the vast timescale of Earth's history, spanning approximately **4.56 billion years**. Geologists organize this history using a **geologic time scale** built from two complementary methods: relative age dating (sequence of events) and numerical/absolute age dating (actual years via radioactive decay).

## Two Types of Dates

| Type | What It Gives | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Relative date** | Sequence: older/younger than | "The Hermit Shale is older than the Coconino Sandstone" |
| **Numerical (absolute) date** | Age in years | "The limestone is 250 million years old" |

## Relative Age Dating Principles

Five principles developed by Steno (1669) and others allow geologists to determine the relative order of rock formation without knowing actual ages.

### 1. Superposition
In an undeformed sedimentary sequence, **lower layers are older**. Also applies to lava flows and ash beds.

### 2. Original Horizontality
Sediment is deposited in **nearly horizontal layers**. Tilted or folded strata were deformed after deposition.

### 3. Lateral Continuity
Sedimentary layers extend **laterally in all directions** until they thin out or change composition. Matching strata on opposite canyon walls were once connected.

### 4. Cross-Cutting Relationships
Any feature (fault, intrusion, erosion surface) that **cuts across** existing rocks is **younger** than the rocks it cuts.

### 5. Inclusions
**Fragments** (xenoliths) of one rock enclosed within another mean the enclosing rock is **younger** — it incorporated pieces of the older rock.

## Unconformities

An **unconformity** is a gap in the rock record representing a period of uplift, erosion, or non-deposition.

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Angular unconformity** | Tilted rocks overlain by flat-lying strata (deformation + erosion + renewed deposition) |
| **Disconformity** | Parallel strata separated by an erosion surface |
| **Nonconformity** | Sedimentary rock overlying igneous or metamorphic basement |

The Grand Canyon exposes all three types.

## Fossils and Correlation

**Fossils** are remains or traces of prehistoric life preserved in rock.

- **Principle of Faunal Succession**: fossil assemblages change through time in a consistent order; each era has characteristic organisms (Age of Trilobites → Age of Fishes → Age of Reptiles → Age of Mammals).
- **Index fossils**: geographically widespread, restricted to a short time span → ideal time markers.
- **Fossil assemblages**: when no single index fossil exists, the combination of species dates the rock.
- Fossils also serve as **environmental indicators** (coral = warm shallow water; certain bivalves = shoreline position).

### Preservation Types
- **Permineralization** — minerals replace or fill porous tissue; "petrified."
- **Molds and casts** — void from dissolved shell; later filled with mineral matter.
- **Carbonization** — thin carbon film after compression; good for leaves and soft organisms.
- **Amber** — hardened tree resin; preserves insects with exceptional detail.
- **Trace fossils** — tracks, burrows, coprolites, gastroliths.

## Numerical (Absolute) Age Dating

### Radioactive Decay
Unstable **parent isotopes** decay spontaneously to stable **daughter isotopes** at a known, constant rate. Types of decay:

- **Alpha decay**: loses 2 protons + 2 neutrons → mass −4, atomic number −2.
- **Beta decay**: electron ejected from neutron → mass unchanged, atomic number +1.
- **Electron capture**: electron absorbed → mass unchanged, atomic number −1.

### Half-Life
The time for **half** the parent atoms to decay. After *n* half-lives, (½)ⁿ of the parent remains.

| Half-lives elapsed | Parent remaining | Daughter present |
|-------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| 0 | 100% | 0% |
| 1 | 50% | 50% |
| 2 | 25% | 75% |
| 3 | 12.5% | 87.5% |
| 4 | 6.25% | 93.75% |

### Common Isotope Pairs

| Parent | Daughter | Half-Life | Best Used For |
|--------|----------|-----------|--------------|
| U-238 | Pb-206 | 4.5 Ga | Ancient igneous/metamorphic rocks |
| U-235 | Pb-207 | 704 Ma | Ancient igneous/metamorphic rocks |
| Th-232 | Pb-208 | 14.1 Ga | Ancient igneous/metamorphic rocks |
| Rb-87 | Sr-87 | 47.0 Ga | Ancient igneous/metamorphic rocks |
| K-40 | Ar-40 | 1.3 Ga | Volcanic rocks |
| C-14 | N-14 | 5,730 yr | Organic material <70,000 yr old |

### Zircon Geochronology
Zircon crystals (common in granites) incorporate U but exclude Pb during crystallization → ideal closed-system radiometric clock. Oldest known mineral: zircon at **4.4 billion years**.

### Sources of Error
System must remain **closed** (no parent/daughter gain or loss). Weathered rock introduces error; fresh, unaltered samples are preferred. U-238 decays through 13 intermediate steps before reaching stable Pb-206.

### Sedimentary Rock Dating
Sedimentary rocks cannot usually be dated directly; ages are **bracketed** by radiometric dates on igneous bodies that intrude or overlie the sedimentary sequence.

## Alternative Methods (Non-Isotopic)
- **Dendrochronology** — tree ring counting.
- **Varves** — annual sediment layers in glacial lakes.
- **Ice cores** — annual ice layers.
- **Growth lines in shells**.
