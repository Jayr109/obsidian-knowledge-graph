**Igneous rock texture** is the size, shape, and arrangement of a rock's mineral grains. Because grain size is governed primarily by **cooling rate**, texture records the environment in which a magma or lava solidified — making it, alongside composition, one of the two pillars for classifying [[Igneous Rocks]].

## The Cooling-Rate Principle

- **Slow cooling** (deep underground) gives ions time to migrate and build **large** crystals → coarse-grained, **intrusive** textures.
- **Fast cooling** (at or near the surface) freezes ions before large crystals form → fine-grained, glassy, or vesicular **extrusive** textures.

## Texture Types

| Texture | Description | Cooling rate | Setting |
|---------|-------------|--------------|---------|
| **Pegmatitic** | Exceptionally coarse crystals | Extremely slow (late-stage, fluid-rich) | Intrusive |
| **Phaneritic** | Coarse — crystals visible to the eye | Slow | Intrusive (deep) |
| **Porphyritic** | Large **phenocrysts** in a finer **groundmass** | Two-stage (slow then fast) | Mixed |
| **Aphanitic** | Fine — crystals microscopic | Fast | Extrusive |
| **Vesicular** | Voids left by escaping gas bubbles | Very fast | Extrusive |
| **Glassy** | No crystals; ions frozen in place (e.g., obsidian) | Extremely fast | Extrusive |
| **Pyroclastic (fragmental)** | Consolidated ejected fragments | Explosive eruption | Extrusive |

## Key Terms

- **Phenocrysts** — the large crystals in a porphyritic rock (grew slowly at depth before eruption).
- **Groundmass** — the fine-grained matrix surrounding the phenocrysts (cooled quickly after eruption). A porphyritic rock is also called a **porphyry**.
- **Pyroclastic** textures connect igneous rocks to volcanic ejecta — see [[Pyroclastic Materials]].
