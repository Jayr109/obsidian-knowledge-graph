The rock cycle describes the continuous processes by which rocks transform from one type into another over geologic time. It is driven by Earth's internal heat and surface processes (weathering, erosion).

## Three Rock Types

### Igneous Rocks
- Formed by the cooling and solidification of magma (intrusive) or lava (extrusive).
- **Intrusive (plutonic)**: slow cooling deep underground → coarse-grained (phaneritic) texture.
- **Extrusive (volcanic)**: fast cooling at surface → fine-grained (aphanitic), glassy, or vesicular texture.
- See [[Igneous Rocks]].

### Sedimentary Rocks
- Formed by accumulation and cementation (lithification) of sediments and smaller rock fragments.
- Three categories:
  - **Clastic**: fragments of pre-existing rocks (sandstone, shale, conglomerate).
  - **Biological (organic)**: formed from remains of organisms (limestone from shells, coal from plants).
  - **Chemical**: precipitated from solution (rock salt, travertine).

### Metamorphic Rocks
- Formed when existing rocks are subjected to intense heat and/or pressure, causing recrystallization without melting.
- **Contact metamorphism**: heat from nearby magma; tends to produce non-foliated textures (small scale).
- **Regional metamorphism**: large-scale heat and pressure from mountain-building; produces foliated textures (e.g., slate, schist, gneiss).
- **Foliated**: minerals aligned in parallel bands (regional metamorphism).
- **Non-foliated**: no preferred mineral orientation (contact metamorphism).

## Cycle Pathways
- Igneous → weathering/erosion → sediment → lithification → Sedimentary.
- Sedimentary → heat + pressure → Metamorphic.
- Metamorphic → melting → Magma → cooling → Igneous.
- Any rock type → melting → Magma → Igneous.
- Any rock type → exposure at surface → weathering → Sedimentary.

## Significance
- Demonstrates that Earth's materials are continuously recycled over geologic time.
- Connects surface processes (weathering, erosion, deposition) with internal processes (magmatism, metamorphism).
- Driven by plate tectonics (subduction recycles crust into mantle; rifting and volcanism bring new material to surface).
