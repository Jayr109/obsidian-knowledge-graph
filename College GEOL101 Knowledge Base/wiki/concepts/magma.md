Magma is completely or partly molten rock at depth in Earth. It is the parent material of all igneous rocks and the source of volcanic activity.

## Components
1. **Melt** — the liquid portion.
2. **Crystals** — silicate minerals (if any have already solidified).
3. **Volatiles** — dissolved gases: H₂O (most common), CO₂, SO₂, H₂S, CO, N₂. Make up 1–8% of magma by weight.

**Lava** = magma that has erupted onto Earth's surface.

## Viscosity
- **Viscosity** = internal resistance to flow.
- Controls eruption style (effusive vs. explosive).
- **Increasing temperature → decreasing viscosity** (hotter = runnier).
- **Increasing silica (SiO₂) content → increasing viscosity** (silica-rich = thicker).
- Basaltic magma: low viscosity (like water) → effusive eruptions.
- Rhyolitic/granitic magma: high viscosity (like honey) → explosive eruptions (gas trapped).

## How Magma Forms (Three Triggers)

### 1. Decompression Melting
- Reducing confining pressure lowers the melting temperature of rock.
- Rising hot mantle rock ascends to lower-pressure regions → melts without adding heat.
- Occurs at: **divergent boundaries** (spreading centers) and **hot spots** (mantle plumes).

### 2. Addition of Water
- Water and other volatiles lower the melting temperature of mantle rock.
- Occurs at **subduction zones**: heat and pressure drive water from subducting oceanic crust + sediments → fluids migrate into overlying mantle wedge → partial melting.
- Produces andesitic to felsic magmas (water from ocean-derived crust enriches the melt).

### 3. Temperature Increase (Heat Transfer)
- Mantle-derived basaltic magma ponds beneath low-density continental crust.
- Heat melts surrounding felsic crustal rocks → felsic magma generation.
- Also occurs during continental collisions.

## Bowen's Reaction Series
- Minerals crystallize from magma in a systematic order based on melting points.
- **Olivine** crystallizes first (highest melting point); **quartz** last (lowest melting point).
- As each mineral crystallizes, the remaining melt becomes depleted in those elements → **fractional crystallization** continuously changes melt composition.
- After ~1/3 crystallizes, remaining melt severely depleted in Fe, Mg, Ca; enriched in Si, K, Na.

## How Magmas Evolve
- **Crystal settling**: early-formed denser minerals sink to magma chamber floor → remaining melt has different composition.
- **Magmatic differentiation**: formation of one or more secondary magmas from a single parent magma (via crystal settling and other processes).
- **Assimilation**: ascending magma incorporates and melts blocks of surrounding country rock → bulk composition shifts.
- **Magma mixing**: two distinct magma bodies merge → intermediate composition.

## Partial Melting
- Most magmas form by **incomplete (partial) melting** — not all rock melts at once.
- Low-melting-point minerals melt first; melt is enriched in those ions.
- Ultramafic (peridotite) partially melts → **mafic (basaltic) magma**.
- Mafic partially melts → **intermediate magma**.
- Intermediate partially melts → **felsic magma**.
- Most erupted magma is basaltic (primary/primitive magma from partial melting of mantle at oceanic ridges).

## Geothermal Gradient
- Temperature increases ~25°C per km in the upper crust.
- Lower crust and upper mantle rocks are near their melting points → small triggers suffice.
