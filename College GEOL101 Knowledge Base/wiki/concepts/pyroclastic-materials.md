**Pyroclastic materials** (collectively **tephra**) are the fragments of pulverized rock, lava, and glass ejected during a volcanic eruption. They are produced when dissolved gases (volatiles, 1–8% of magma by weight) expand violently as magma nears the surface, and they are the building blocks of composite volcanoes, cinder cones, and pyroclastic [[Igneous Rocks]] like tuff. They are central to the explosive side of [[Volcanoes]].

## Types of Tephra

| Material | Size / Description |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Volcanic ash** | Fine glassy fragments; can fuse into **welded tuff** |
| **Lapilli / cinders** | Walnut-sized fragments |
| **Blocks** | Hardened/cooled lava >2.5 inches; angular (ejected solid) |
| **Bombs** | Ejected as hot, molten lava >2.5 inches; streamlined shape acquired in flight |
| **Pumice** | Light gray/pink, porous rock from frothy andesitic/rhyolitic lava (floats on water) |
| **Scoria** | Reddish-brown, porous rock from frothy basaltic lava |

## Why It Matters

- **Explosiveness** is set by magma chemistry: high-silica, high-viscosity felsic magmas trap gas and fragment violently, producing abundant tephra; low-viscosity basaltic magmas release gas quietly (see [[Magma]]).
- Pyroclastic materials accumulate to build **cinder cones** and the alternating layers of **composite volcanoes**.
- They drive some of the deadliest [[Volcanic Hazards]] — **pyroclastic flows** (nuée ardente), **ash fall** (roof collapse, crop loss, jet-engine damage), and, when remobilized by water, **lahars**.
