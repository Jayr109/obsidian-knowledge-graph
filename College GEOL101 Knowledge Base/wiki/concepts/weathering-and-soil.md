Weathering is the physical breakdown and chemical alteration of rock at or near Earth's surface. It is the first step in the formation of [[Sedimentary Rocks]] and generates the regolith from which soil develops. Two types always work simultaneously and reinforce each other.

## Mechanical Weathering (Disintegration)

Physical forces break rock into smaller pieces without changing its chemical composition. The main effect is **increasing surface area** for chemical attack.

### Types
| Process | Mechanism | Environment | Primary Form |
|---------|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| **Frost Wedging** | Freeze/thaw of water in cracks | High latitude/elevation, tundra | Shattering, block separation |
| **Salt Crystal Growth** | Crystals grow in cracks (halite, gypsum) exert pressure | Arid environments, rocky coasts | Granular disintegration |
| **Sheeting / Unloading** | Deeply buried rock exhumed by erosion; stress release | Massive rocks (granite) | Exfoliation, block separation |
| **Hydration** | Wetting/drying; no chemical change | Varied | Shattering, block separation |
| **Biological Activity** | Root wedging; burrowing animals; organic acids from decay | Varied | Varied |

### Weathering Geometries
Granular disintegration, spheroidal weathering, exfoliation, block separation, shattering.

## Chemical Weathering (Decomposition)

Chemical transformation of rock minerals into new compounds. Most important agent: **water**.

### Processes
- **Dissolution** — minerals dissolve into solution.
  - *Simple solution*: halite dissolves directly; reversible by evaporation.
  - *Carbonation*: CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid) attacks calcite/limestone; responsible for cave formation.
    - CaCO₃ + H₂CO₃ → Ca²⁺ + 2HCO₃⁻
- **Oxidation** — metals (most commonly Fe) combine with oxygen; produces iron oxide colors (reds, browns, yellows).
- **Hydrolysis** — chemical reaction between water and silicate minerals; most common in granite; byproduct is **clay minerals**.
  - 2KAlSi₃O₈ + 2H⁺ + 9H₂O → Kaolinite + Silicic Acid + K⁺ (feldspar → kaolinite clay)

### Rates of Chemical Weathering
- Controlled by rock characteristics (mineral composition, solubility) and climate (temperature, precipitation).
- Silicate minerals weather in the reverse order of Bowen's Reaction Series — minerals that crystallize last (quartz) are most resistant.
- **Differential weathering** — rock masses weather unevenly depending on composition and fracture patterns.
- Acid rain accelerates chemical weathering on human structures and natural rock.

## Soil

Soil is the portion of **regolith** (weathered rock/mineral layer at Earth's surface) that supports plant growth.
- ~50% disintegrated rock + mineral matter + **humus** (organic matter from decomposed plants/animals).

### Controls of Soil Formation (five factors)
1. **Parent material** — determines chemical composition and fertility; residual soils (from bedrock) vs. transported soils (from sediment).
2. **Climate** — most influential factor; temperature + precipitation control weathering rate; determines vegetation.
3. **Plants, Animals, and Microbes** — primary organic matter source; **bioturbation** (earthworm/burrowing mixing).
4. **Time** — longer weathering → thicker soil with less resemblance to parent material.
5. **Topography** — steep slopes → thin, poorly developed soils (rapid runoff); flat/undulating → optimal; south-facing slopes (N. Hemisphere) receive most sunlight → optimal.

### Soil Profile (Horizons)

| Horizon | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **O** | Organic matter (plant material + decomposing matter); topsoil |
| **A** | Organic + mineral matter; high biological activity; topsoil |
| **E** | Light-colored; eluviation (fine components washed down); leaching |
| **B (subsoil)** | Zone of accumulation; receives leached material from E |
| **C** | Partially altered parent material |

### Soil Erosion
- Natural process accelerated by deforestation and farming.
- Sequence: sheet erosion → rills → gullies (cannot be remediated by normal cultivation).
- **1930s Dust Bowl**: drought + unprotected soils in Great Plains (OK, TX, KS) → catastrophic wind erosion.
- Conservation methods: terracing on steep slopes, strip cropping, grassed drainage ways.

### Soil Texture
- Refers to proportions of different particle sizes (sand, silt, clay).
- Influences water retention, air flow, and cultivation ease.
- Four basic soil structures: platy, prismatic, blocky, spheroidal.
