Session levels are the notable highs and lows formed during prior trading sessions (Asian and London) that have not yet been traded through. They act as reference levels for the following US session and are used as confluence points for entries, stop placement, and target selection.

## Sessions and Approximate Times (ET / CST)

| Session | ET | CST |
|---------|-----|------|
| Asia | 6:00 PM – 3:00 AM | 5:00 PM – 2:00 AM |
| London | 3:00 AM – 11:30 AM | 2:00 AM – 10:30 AM |
| US | 8:30 AM – 4:00 PM | 7:30 AM – 3:00 PM |

## Why Session Levels Matter

Institutional and algorithmic participants track prior session highs and lows because they represent:
- **Liquidity pools:** Stop orders cluster just above session highs and below session lows.
- **Reference points:** Large participants use prior session ranges to frame value and direction.
- **Magnet levels:** Price frequently reaches for untested prior session extremes before reversing or continuing.

"Untested" means price has not yet traded to that level in the current or subsequent session. Once a level is tested (price reaches it), it is no longer tracked as an active session level.

## Application in Strategies

**[[7AM Zone and Session Levels]] (MES1):**
- Asian and London highs/lows are marked before the US open.
- Entries near untested session levels carry higher conviction — the zone setup aligns with a level the market is already attracted to.
- Session levels function as both targets (price is moving toward them) and entry filters (retest near a level is more reliable).

## How to Identify

- Mark the highest high and lowest low formed during each session on the chart.
- Do not include pre-open or after-hours prints outside regular session hours.
- Carry levels forward until they are tested.
