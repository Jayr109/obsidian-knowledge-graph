An **organization** is a group of people working together in a structured and coordinated fashion to achieve a set of goals. It is the entity that [[Management]] acts upon, and the definition is the logical starting point of the course — management only makes sense as management *of* something.

## The Three Conditions

The definition contains three requirements, and all must hold:

| Element | What it rules out |
|---|---|
| **A group of people** | An individual working alone is not an organization |
| **Structured and coordinated** | A crowd or an unorganized collection of people is not an organization |
| **A set of goals** | A group with no shared purpose is not an organization |

Structure and coordination are what distinguish an organization from a mere group. That distinction is what creates the need for managers in the first place: coordination does not happen spontaneously at scale, so someone must plan, organize, lead, and control it — the [[Basic Management Functions]].

## Goals as the Reference Point

Goals appear throughout the chapter as the fixed point against which everything is measured:

- Effectiveness in [[Management]] is defined relative to making the *right* decisions — that is, decisions that serve the goals.
- Planning is defined as setting goals and deciding how to achieve them.
- Controlling is defined as monitoring progress *toward goal attainment*.

An organization without goals would leave three of the four management functions undefined.

## Organizations as Systems

The [[Systems Perspective]] later in the chapter re-describes the organization as a system: a set of interrelated elements functioning as a whole, taking inputs from an environment and returning outputs to it. That view is what makes concepts like open vs. closed systems, synergy, and entropy applicable to organizations at all.
