The chapter closes with the major challenges and opportunities facing managers today. Several are framed deliberately as **both** challenge and opportunity rather than as threats alone.

## The Challenges

| Challenge | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Globalization** | Explicitly both a challenge *and* an opportunity |
| **Business scandals** | Unethical conduct in business |
| **Quality** | A basis for competition; improving it tends to raise productivity and lower costs |
| **Shift to a service economy** | Movement away from a manufacturing-centered economy |
| **Economic recession** | The 2008–2010 recession and slow 2011–2017 recovery created wide-ranging challenges |
| **Changing workplace** | Management of diversity — ethnic and demographic composition of the workforce |
| **Organization change** | Ongoing structural and strategic change as a permanent condition |
| **Social media** | Noted specifically for its *negative* impact in business |

## Quality: Three Reasons It Matters

Quality gets the most developed treatment, with three distinct arguments:

1. It is used as a **basis for competition** — a market differentiator.
2. Improving quality tends to **increase productivity**.
3. Enhancing quality **lowers costs**.

The second and third are the counterintuitive ones. The naive view treats quality as a cost center — something you buy more of by spending more. The chapter's claim is the opposite: quality improvement pays for itself through fewer defects, less rework, and less waste. This is the standard quality-management argument and is likely to be tested.

## Diversity as a Management Task

Diversity is presented under the *changing workplace* heading and framed as something to be **managed** — with ethnic and demographic composition named explicitly. The framing is managerial rather than merely descriptive: the workforce is changing, and responding to that change is part of the job.

## Connection to the Contingency Perspective

This closing list is the practical argument for the [[Contingency Perspective]]. Globalization, a shifting economy, a changing workforce, and continuous organizational change all mean the situation a manager faces is unstable and varied. Universal prescriptions from the [[Classical Management Perspective]] — built for *stable, simple organizations* — cannot accommodate that. The list of contemporary challenges is, in effect, the evidence that contingency thinking is required.

It also reinforces the [[Systems Perspective]]: every item here is an **environmental** factor. An organization treated as a closed system would have no way to register any of them.
