**Management** is a set of activities directed at an organization's resources with the aim of achieving organizational goals in an efficient and effective manner. A **manager** is someone whose primary responsibility is to carry out the management process.

The definition is compact but every clause carries weight, and the course tests it clause by clause: management acts on *resources*, aims at *organizational goals*, and must satisfy *both* efficiency and effectiveness.

## Efficient vs. Effective

The single most confusable pair in the chapter, and the one most likely to appear on an exam.

| Term | Definition | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Efficient** | Using resources wisely and in a cost-effective way | Are we doing things *right*? |
| **Effective** | Making the right decisions and successfully implementing them | Are we doing the *right things*? |

Both are required. An organization can be efficient without being effective — executing flawlessly on the wrong goal — or effective without being efficient, reaching the right goal at ruinous cost. The definition of management demands both simultaneously.

## Resources

Management is defined as acting on an organization's resources. These are the inputs the manager combines and deploys — human, financial, physical, and informational — and the manager's job is to convert them into goal attainment. The functional specializations in [[Kinds of Managers]] map fairly directly onto which resource a manager principally handles.

## Why It Matters

The chapter makes an unusually strong claim about managerial impact: good managers can propel an organization into unprecedented realms of success, while poor managers can devastate even the strongest organization. Management is presented not as administrative overhead but as a primary determinant of organizational outcome.
