The management process consists of **four basic functions**: planning and decision making, organizing, leading, and controlling. This four-part scheme is the backbone of the course — most later chapters expand one of these four, and the list should be memorized complete and in order.

## The Four Functions

### 1. Planning and Decision Making

- **Planning**: setting an organization's goals and deciding how best to achieve them.
- **Decision making**: part of the planning process; selecting a course of action from a set of alternatives.

Plans help maintain managerial effectiveness by serving as guides for future activity. Note that decision making is presented as a *component of* planning here, not as a fifth co-equal function — though it recurs as its own skill in [[Fundamental Management Skills]] and as the focus of the [[Quantitative Management Perspective]].

### 2. Organizing

Determining how to group activities and resources.

This is the structural function: once goals and plans exist, someone must decide who does what, in what groupings, with which resources.

### 3. Leading

The set of processes used to get members of the organization to work together to further the interests of the organization.

Leading is the people function. Its intellectual ancestry is the [[Behavioral Management Perspective]], and it is where motivation, group dynamics, and communication live.

### 4. Controlling

Monitoring organizational progress toward goal attainment.

As the organization moves toward its goals, managers must monitor progress. Controlling closes the loop back to planning: it compares actual progress against the goals set in step 1, which is why the four functions are usually drawn as a cycle rather than a straight line.

## Why the Order Matters

The sequence is not arbitrary. Planning sets the goals; organizing arranges resources to pursue them; leading mobilizes people to execute; controlling measures whether the goals are being met — and feeds any shortfall back into planning. Goals, defined in [[Organization]], are the thread running through all four.

## Functions vs. Skills

A common point of confusion. **Functions are what managers do; skills are what managers must be good at.** The four functions are activities in a process; the seven items in [[Fundamental Management Skills]] are capabilities. They are different lists answering different questions, and exam items sometimes mix items from the two.
