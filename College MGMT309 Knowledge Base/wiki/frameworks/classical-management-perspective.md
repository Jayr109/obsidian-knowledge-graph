The **classical management perspective** is the earliest of the three historical schools, and splits into two branches: **scientific management**, concerned with improving the performance of individual workers, and **administrative management**, concerned with managing the total organization. It is the first of the three perspectives covered in [[The Evolution of Management Thought]].

## The Two Branches

| Branch | Unit of analysis | Key figures |
|---|---|---|
| **Scientific management** | The individual worker | [[Frederick W. Taylor]] |
| **Administrative management** | The total organization | [[Henri Fayol]], [[Max Weber]] |

The split is essentially one of scope — bottom-up versus top-down — and the two branches developed largely in parallel rather than in sequence.

## Scientific Management

Concerned with improving the performance of individual workers.

**Soldiering** — employees deliberately working at a slow pace — is the problem scientific management set out to solve. [[Frederick W. Taylor]] implemented a **piecework pay system** in response: pay tied to output rather than to time, so that working slowly no longer paid.

The underlying assumption is that worker behavior responds to economic incentive, and that the right incentive structure plus the right method will maximize output. That assumption is precisely what the [[Hawthorne Studies]] later called into question.

## Administrative Management

Focuses on managing the total organization.

- [[Henri Fayol]] attempted to systematize management practice, providing guidance and direction to other managers — an effort to make management teachable rather than purely experiential.
- [[Max Weber]]'s work on **bureaucracy** laid the foundation for contemporary organization theory.

## The Classical Perspective Today

The chapter pairs each perspective with a critique, and this one has three specific limitations:

1. **Focused on stable, simple organizations** — the assumption of a predictable environment does not survive contact with modern conditions.
2. **Proposed universal guidelines that we now recognize do not fit every organization** — this is exactly the [[Contingency Perspective|universal perspective]] that the contingency view later rejects.
3. **Slighted the role of the individual in organizations** — treating workers as inputs to be optimized, the gap that the [[Behavioral Management Perspective]] was formed to fill.

Each limitation points forward to a later school. The critiques are not incidental; they are the reason the subsequent perspectives exist.
