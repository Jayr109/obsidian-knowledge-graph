Douglas McGregor formulated **Theory X and Theory Y**, the contrasting sets of assumptions about human nature at work that became the signature contribution of the human relations movement within the [[Behavioral Management Perspective]].

## Contribution

McGregor's framework appears in *The Human Side of Enterprise* (1960), cited in the chapter in the 25th Anniversary Printing (McGraw Hill), co-credited with W. Bennis.

The insight is second-order: rather than claiming to describe what workers are actually like, McGregor described **what managers assume** workers are like, and showed that those assumptions shape managerial behavior — and thus shape the outcomes that appear to confirm them.

See [[Theory X and Theory Y]] for the full contrast.

## Placement in the History

The chapter aligns Theory X with the views of scientific management, which makes McGregor's framework a diagnostic tool for the whole historical arc in [[The Evolution of Management Thought]]: the [[Classical Management Perspective]] operates on Theory X assumptions, the behavioral perspective on Theory Y.
