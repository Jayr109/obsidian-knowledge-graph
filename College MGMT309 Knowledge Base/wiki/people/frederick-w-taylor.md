Frederick W. Taylor is the central figure of **scientific management**, the branch of the [[Classical Management Perspective]] concerned with improving the performance of individual workers.

## Contribution

Taylor addressed **soldiering** — employees deliberately working at a slow pace — by implementing a **piecework pay system**, tying compensation to output rather than to time worked. Under piecework, working slowly no longer pays, so the incentive to soldier is removed.

## The Assumption Underneath

Taylor's system rests on the premise that worker behavior responds to economic incentive: set the reward correctly and output follows. That premise is what the [[Hawthorne Studies]] directly contradicted, when a group under a piecework plan informally capped its own output and socially punished the *rate busters* who exceeded the cap.

The chapter also aligns Taylor's outlook with **Theory X** — the pessimistic view of workers as work-averse and requiring control. See [[Theory X and Theory Y]].

## Legacy

Scientific management's ambition to find the one best way to perform a task is the clearest example of the **universal perspective** that the [[Contingency Perspective]] later rejects.
