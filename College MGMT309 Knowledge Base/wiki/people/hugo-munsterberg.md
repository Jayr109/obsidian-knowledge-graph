Hugo Münsterberg (1863–1916) is known as the **father of industrial psychology** and is an early contributor to the [[Behavioral Management Perspective]].

## Contribution

Münsterberg suggested that psychologists could make valuable contributions to managers in three areas:

- Employee **selection**
- **Training**
- **Motivation**

## Significance

The claim was methodological as much as substantive: it proposed that a scientific discipline concerned with the human mind had something to offer management. That is the opening move of the entire behavioral tradition, which the [[Classical Management Perspective]] had left unaddressed by slighting the role of the individual.

All three of Münsterberg's areas persist as named topics in modern practice — selection and training belong to human resource management (see [[Kinds of Managers]]), and motivation is a core subject of organizational behavior.
