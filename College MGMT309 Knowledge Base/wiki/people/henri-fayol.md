Henri Fayol is a principal figure in **administrative management**, the branch of the [[Classical Management Perspective]] that focuses on managing the total organization rather than the individual worker.

## Contribution

Fayol attempted to **systematize management practice** in order to provide guidance and direction to other managers. The significance of the effort is that it treated management as a body of transmissible knowledge — something that could be taught and written down — rather than as a purely personal craft acquired only by experience.

That premise is what makes a course like [[MGMT 309]] possible in the first place.

## Relationship to the Later Schools

Fayol's general principles were **universalist** in ambition: guidance intended to apply to managers generally. This is precisely the stance the [[Contingency Perspective]] rejects, and it is why the chapter lists "proposed universal guidelines that we now recognize do not fit every organization" among the classical perspective's limitations.

The ambition survives even where the universalism does not — [[Basic Management Functions]] is itself a systematized account of what managers do, in the Fayol tradition.
