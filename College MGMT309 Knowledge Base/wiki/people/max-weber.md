Max Weber contributed to **administrative management** within the [[Classical Management Perspective]] through his work on **bureaucracy**, which laid the foundation for contemporary organization theory.

## Contribution

Weber's analysis of bureaucracy is the origin point for the systematic study of organizational structure — hierarchy, division of labor, rules, and impersonal authority as deliberate design features rather than accidents of growth.

The chapter credits this work with founding **contemporary organization theory**, making Weber's influence the longest-lived of the classical contributors: the structure and design of organizations remains a named topic within organizational behavior today. See [[Behavioral Management Perspective]].

## A Note on the Word

In everyday use "bureaucracy" is pejorative — a byword for red tape. In Weber's technical sense it is a **neutral description of an organizational form** defined by rules, hierarchy, and impersonal authority. The course uses the technical sense, and conflating it with the everyday sense will produce wrong answers.
