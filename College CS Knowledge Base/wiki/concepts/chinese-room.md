The Chinese Room is John Searle's thought experiment against strong AI. A person who does not understand Chinese sits in a room with a rulebook for manipulating Chinese symbols. Chinese questions come in, the person applies the rules mechanically, and fluent Chinese answers go out. From outside, the room passes for a Chinese speaker; inside, nobody understands anything.

## The Argument

Searle's conclusion: **syntax is not sufficient for semantics.** Executing a program that manipulates symbols correctly does not produce understanding of what those symbols mean. Since that is all a digital computer does, no program can constitute a mind — however well it performs.

This is a direct attack on the [[Turing Test]]. The room is constructed to pass a behavioral test while stipulating that the inner state the test is supposed to certify is absent.

## Course Positioning

Lecture 1 places the Chinese Room on the "Philosophy and AI" slide with the weak/strong contrast stated as:

- **Weak AI:** machines could act *as if* they were intelligent.
- **Strong AI:** machines that do so are *actually consciously thinking* — not just simulating thinking.

The same slide moves directly to **AI ethics** — safety, fairness, privacy, promoting collaboration, transparency, limiting harmful use — treating the philosophical and the ethical questions as one course thread.
