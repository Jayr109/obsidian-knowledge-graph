Lecture 1 tells the history of AI as a sequence of eras, each defined by where knowledge comes from: hand-coded by humans, then learned from structured data, then learned from unstructured data, then learned end-to-end by deep networks. The through-line is a steady retreat of hand-engineering.

## Timeline

**Gestation (1943–1956)**
- McCulloch and Pitts: early neural nets (1943)
- Newell and Simon: physical symbol system hypothesis — Logic Theorist (1956)
- **Dartmouth Workshop (1956): AI was born**

**Early successes (1952–1969)**
- General Problem Solver (1959)
- McCarthy: LISP (1956–1962)
- Rosenblatt: perceptrons (1958)
- Widrow and Hoff: adalines (1960)
- Toy domains: ANALOGY (1968), STUDENT (algebra, 1964)
- Minsky and Papert: limitations of the perceptron (1969)

**60s–70s — mostly hard-coded**
- ELIZA: pattern-matching NLP (1966)
- Genetic algorithms (started 1950s, came of age in the 1960s)
- Knowledge-based systems: scientific, engineering, and natural-language domains

**80s — symbolic logic-based**
- 5th generation AI — Prolog
- Neural networks: Neocognitron, convolutional neural networks, backpropagation

**90s — learning from data**
- Probabilistic approaches: [[Bayesian Networks]], hidden Markov models
- Support vector machines
- Machine learning comes to center stage
- Intelligent agents; statistical NLP

**2000s–early 2010s — learning from unstructured data**
- Big data, web, information retrieval
- IBM Watson (2010)
- Human-based computation: reCAPTCHA, Amazon Mechanical Turk

**Mid 2010s–present — age of deep learning**
- Vision: convolutional neural networks
- Speech: recurrent neural networks
- Natural language: RNNs, attention-based models (Transformer)
- [[Reinforcement Learning|Deep reinforcement learning]]: complex games, robot control, autonomous driving
- Generative AI: large language models, image synthesis
- AGI directions: reasoning, common sense, [[Neurosymbolic AI]]

## The 50th Anniversary Benchmark

At AI@50 (2006), Rodney Brooks characterized the state of the art as a system with:

- the social sophistication of a 10-year-old
- the manual dexterity of a 6-year-old
- the language ability of a 4-year-old
- the visual object recognition of a 2-year-old

The lecture's own 2023 update: vision and language have advanced greatly since 2006; manual dexterity somewhat improved; **social sophistication still a long way to go.** The ordering is worth noting — the abilities that fell fastest are the ones with abundant data and a clear loss function.

## Major Achievements

- AI in computer games (1980s–current)
- Game playing: IBM's Deep Blue beats Kasparov (1997)
- Jeopardy: IBM's Watson (2011)
- DeepMind's AlphaGo beats a Go master 4-1 (2016)
- DeepMind's AlphaStar beats top StarCraft players 10-1 (2019)
- Hinton, LeCun, and Bengio win the 2018 ACM Turing Award (2019)
- Large language models (OpenAI GPT-3, 2020)
- DeepMind's AlphaFold (2021)
- OpenAI's DALL·E 2 (2021)
- GitHub Copilot (2021)
- ChatGPT (OpenAI, 2022)

## Three-Era Summary

| Era | Period | Character |
|-----|--------|-----------|
| [[Symbolic AI]] | 1950s – mid 80s | Symbolic logic, rule-based, theorem proving, expert systems |
| Machine learning | mid 80s – mid 2010s | Probability-based, learn from data, unstructured data, decline of logic-based AI |
| [[Deep Learning]] | mid 2010s – present | CNN, RNN, attention, deep RL; superhuman on some tasks |
