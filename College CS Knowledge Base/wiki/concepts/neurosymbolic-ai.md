Neurosymbolic AI is the program of combining [[Symbolic AI]] with [[Deep Learning]] so that each supplies what the other lacks: neural networks provide perception, grounding, and tolerance for vague concepts; symbolic systems provide reasoning, compositionality, and interpretability. Lecture 1 lists it first among the future directions for the field.

## The Case For It

The two paradigms fail in complementary ways.

| Paradigm | Strength | Failure |
|----------|----------|---------|
| [[Symbolic AI]] | Reasoning, theorem proving, explicit knowledge | Cannot deal with vague concepts; cannot learn from data |
| [[Deep Learning]] | Perception, learning from raw data, superhuman on narrow tasks | Needs large data; cannot do complex reasoning; lacks common sense; not interpretable; learns spurious features |

Neither list is a subset of the other, which is the whole argument.

## Future of AI (Lecture 1, final slide)

The closing slide lists four directions:

1. **Combining symbolic AI and deep learning** — neurosymbolic AI
2. **New methods inspired by neuroscience** — neuroscience-inspired AI, neuromorphic AI
3. **Neural nets + evolution** — neuroevolution
4. **Philosophical issues** — consciousness, epistemology, post-humanism

Note that (4) returns the course to where it started, in [[Philosophy of Mind]] and [[Strong AI and Weak AI]]. The framing is deliberate: the closing slide is the opening question with better tools.

The AGI thread in the [[History of AI]] timeline names the same targets — reasoning, common sense, neurosymbolic AI — as what is missing between current systems and general intelligence.
