The mind-body problem asks how mental phenomena relate to physical ones. It is background for AI because your answer determines whether building a mind out of matter is even coherent. Course reference: B&S pp. 18–19.

## Positions

- **Dualism** — mind and body are separate entities.
- **Monism** — only mind *or* body exists, not both:
  1. **Idealism** — all things are mental.
  2. **Materialism** — all things are material.
- **Epiphenomenalism** — mental phenomena are side-effects of physical change in the brain. They have no causal power over behavior, like the smoke coming off a steam engine.

The lecture notes there are far too many variations to list them all.

## Relevance to AI

Materialism is the implicit working assumption of strong AI — "humans are no more than elaborate computers" is a materialist claim. If dualism holds, no arrangement of matter produces a mind and strong AI is impossible in principle. Epiphenomenalism is the awkward middle: mental states exist but do no causal work, so a system could reproduce all of human behavior with or without them, and no behavioral test could tell the difference — which is the [[Turing Test]] problem restated.
