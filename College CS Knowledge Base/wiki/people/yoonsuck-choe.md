Yoonsuck Choe is the instructor for [[CSCE 420]] (Artificial Intelligence) at Texas A&M. He also authored the course's [[Self-Organizing Map]] demonstration notebook (dated 1/7/2022).

## Perspective on AI

Lecture 1 sets out his stated research perspective, which shapes how the course frames the field:

- Importance of studying **brain function** as a guide to building intelligence
- Influence of **environmental regularities** on brain development and function
- **Interaction** of the brain with the environment through action
- **Prediction**, and exploiting the external medium
- **Tool construction and tool use**

His framing argument is the "plane model" critique: engineering flight worked because the goal was simple and verifiable, whereas intelligence is a collection of many unclearly defined abilities. He illustrates with insect flight — flapping alone cannot generate enough lift for a fly's body weight, yet flies fly by gyrating their wings to generate a lift-enhancing vortex (Ellington et al., *Nature* 384(6610), 626–630, 1996). The moral he draws: **if you fail to build the impossible, study an existing solution.**

This bias toward biological grounding shows up later in the course as sustained attention to sub-symbolic methods — [[Self-Organizing Map]], recurrent nets, episodic memory — and to [[Neurosymbolic AI]] as a direction for the field.
