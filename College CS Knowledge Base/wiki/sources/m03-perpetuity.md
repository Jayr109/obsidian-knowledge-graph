This transcript derives the present value of a [[Perpetuity]] using the historical example of British "consol bonds" issued in 1927 to repay World War One debt. The derivation is a single step of eighth-grade algebra.

## Historical Motivation

In 1927 the British government issued perpetual securities (consol bonds) offering 4% annual payments forever. An investor paying £1,000 received £40/year indefinitely, passing to heirs after death. This is a perpetuity: a fixed payment made at regular intervals forever.

## Derivation

The annual payment equals the amount invested times the interest rate:
```
PMT = PV × i
```
Solving for PV by dividing both sides by i:
```
PV = PMT / i
```
Plugging in: PV = 40 / 0.04 = £1,000. The present value equals the original investment, confirming the formula.

## Key Takeaway

The perpetuity formula `PV = PMT / i` is one of the two foundational formulas for all time-value-of-money analysis. See [[M3: Annuity]] for how the annuity formula is derived from it.
