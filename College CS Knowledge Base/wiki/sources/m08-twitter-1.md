This transcript uses the Elon Musk/Twitter acquisition of 2022 as a real-world test case for the [[Efficient Market Hypothesis]] (EMH). Part 1 reviews the three forms of market efficiency before describing the instructor's personal trading experience.

## Three Forms of the EMH

**Strong form:** All information — public *and* private — is reflected in prices. Even insider information and concealed fraud would be priced in. This form is rejected by evidence (e.g., Enron: stock did not price in the massive accounting fraud until it was exposed; SEC insider trading prosecutions show private info *does* move prices).

**Semi-strong form:** All *publicly available* information is reflected in prices. Prices update instantly when new public information arrives. This implies you cannot profit from reading financial statements, news, or M&A filings — they're already priced.

**Weak form:** All information in *past prices and volume* is reflected in current prices. You cannot use technical analysis (chart patterns, volume indicators) to predict future returns beyond what is expected as risk compensation. Notably, using past prices to *estimate beta* and predict higher average returns for high-beta stocks does NOT violate weak-form efficiency — that is compensation for risk.
