This transcript narrates the instructor's personal experience trading Twitter stock during the 2022 Musk acquisition, presenting evidence that seemed to contradict semi-strong form EMH — yet also constructing the counter-argument.

## Timeline of Events

| Date | Event | Price |
|---|---|---|
| April 14, 2022 | Musk offers $54.20/share | Jumps slightly |
| April 25, 2022 | Twitter accepts offer | ~$49 (not $54.20) |
| April 29, 2022 | Binding acquisition signed | ~$49 |
| May 3, 2022 | Instructor buys at ~$49 | $49 |
| May 13, 2022 | Musk declares deal "on hold" | Drops |
| May 16, 2022 | Price falls further | ~$37 |
| May 18, 2022 | Instructor doubles down at ~$37 | $37 |
| July 8, 2022 | Musk declares deal off | Drops further |
| July 12, 2022 | Twitter sues Musk | Rises slightly |
| Oct 3, 2022 | Musk agrees to proceed | Jumps |
| Oct 27, 2022 | Deal closes at $54.20 | Market price still $53.70 at 4pm |

## The Apparent EMH Violation

Even with a *binding contract* to pay $54.20/share — including:
- Musk's explicit waiver of due diligence rights
- Binding bank financing commitments
- Legal commentary overwhelmingly favoring Twitter's case

…the market priced Twitter at $49, then $37. The price consistently underreflected publicly available information, suggesting semi-strong inefficiency.

## The Counter-Argument

The instructor requires students to argue **both sides** in a forum discussion:
- **Against EMH:** Price persistently below deal value despite clear public information.
- **For EMH:** Perhaps investors rationally discounted the real legal uncertainty — sophisticated arbitrageurs may have priced tail risks the instructor underweighted. Behavioral biases (his own loss aversion after the price drop) also complicate interpretation.

## Behavioral Finance Note

The instructor acknowledges his own **loss aversion bias**: once prices fell after he bought, he "chickened out" and didn't buy more even though his analysis hadn't changed — a classic behavioral finance example.
