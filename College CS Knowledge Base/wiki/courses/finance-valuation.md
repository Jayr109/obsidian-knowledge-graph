Corporate Finance and Valuation is an undergraduate finance course that teaches students how to value financial assets and make investment decisions using rigorous, cash-flow-based methods. The course emphasizes the time value of money, risk measurement, and the supremacy of the NPV rule for capital budgeting.

## Course Overview

The course spans modules covering foundational math of finance through advanced valuation topics. The central theme is that **the value of any financial instrument is a function of its expected future cash flows, the timing of those cash flows, and their riskiness**. This principle ties together every topic from perpetuities to free cash flow analysis.

## Core Thesis

The course repeatedly returns to one principle: **use NPV**. The NPV rule is the only investment decision rule that directly measures the degree to which an investment increases the value of a business. IRR, payback period, and PE ratios are all useful approximations or supplementary tools, but none substitutes for proper DCF analysis grounded in free cash flow projections.

## Key Formulas

- **Perpetuity PV:** `PV = PMT / i`
- **Annuity PV:** `PV = PMT/i − (PMT/i) × 1/(1+i)^N`
- **Effective Annual Rate:** `EAR = (1 + periodic rate)^M − 1`
- **Fischer Equation (exact):** `(1 + r_nominal) = (1 + r_real)(1 + π_expected)`
- **Free Cash Flow:** `FCF = EBIT(1−t) + D&A − ΔGFA − ΔNWC`

## Textbook Reference

The instructor references a standard corporate finance textbook with chapter assignments (e.g., Chapter 2 for accounting review, Section 8.4 for PE ratios, page 44/337 for FCF formula). The course supplements the textbook with conceptual derivations the book omits.
